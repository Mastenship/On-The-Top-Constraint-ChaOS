# Substrate Physics: The Geometric Language of Governance Coupling

## 1. Where This Paper Picks Up

Two prior documents establish the foundation this paper builds on, and naming them precisely is the first step.

The philosophy paper establishes *where* governance must live. The interior of an opaque generative system is not accessible to outside observers in the way governance requires; the only available site at which governance can operate is the boundary where semantic generation becomes consequential state. This is the central thesis of the Mutation Boundary Problem and the foundation OTTC is built on. The boundary is not a stopgap pending better interior access. It is the constitutionally correct location for governance of opaque generators, and the alternatives — informal mutation, hidden state, prose-level accountability — are forms of the same governance problem solved less well rather than alternatives that bypass it.

The dual-readership document establishes *how* the boundary must be addressed. OTTC governance is delivered through text. Text competes for attention. The governance surface must therefore be engineered to win attention competition against the model's default continuation tendencies — through canonical tokens that are tokenically alien to the prose distribution, through structural markers that segment governance from content, and through constitutional locality that places the write contract at every site where proposals are generated, because long-range attention is unreliable. The principle is stated compactly: structurally distinct tokens function as high-salience attention anchors; constitutional repetition keeps governance in local context; coupling cannot be assumed and must be measured per substrate. The principle is correct, and the corpus operates under it consistently.

What is missing between these two documents is the description language for *what is happening in the substrate* when text-as-governance encounters a transformer's continuation dynamics. The philosophy paper supplies the constitutional foundation. The dual-readership document supplies the engineering principle. Neither develops the vocabulary in which a practitioner can *reason about* the architecture's behavior on the substrate — the language for thinking about why a particular rendering might bind trajectories on one substrate and slip on another, why constitutional locality is paying for itself in field strength rather than wasting context budget, why assay results vary in the patterns they do, why some governance moves are safe to promote to hard enforcement and others are not.

This paper supplies that language. It does not introduce a new doctrine. It develops a geometric vocabulary — mass, fields, perceived slopes, anti-mass — that the existing doctrines already imply but do not name. Two distinctions are worth marking explicitly at the outset, because the vocabulary's usefulness depends on keeping them in view.

What is genuinely the case about the substrate, independent of any vocabulary choice: the continuation distribution has structure that varies with context; tokens differentially condition next-token probability; placing tokens in context modifies that probability landscape in ways that vary by substrate. These are descriptions of what transformer inference is doing, not metaphors for it. They are also describable in many ways — as attention dynamics, as conditional distributions, as activation patterns under specific inputs.

What is a vocabulary choice rather than a structural claim: calling the differential conditioning "mass," the resulting probability landscape "a field," the substrate-relative experience of that landscape a "perceived slope." This vocabulary borrows from physics. It is one description language among several that could capture the same underlying mechanics. The justification for using it is practical: the geometric vocabulary makes certain design-decision implications more legible than the existing language does. It is not the unique correct description language for the phenomenon, and the paper does not claim that it is. What the paper claims is that the vocabulary fits the phenomenon's gradient-based and observer-relative character closely enough to be useful, and that practitioners who internalize it gain reasoning power they previously had to reach for case by case.

The paper is a corpus extension, not a re-introduction. It assumes the reader knows what the mutation boundary is, what canonical addressing does, and why governance lives at the boundary. It does not re-argue the foundational thesis, re-introduce OTTC's architecture, or rehearse the institutional-continuity argument from the philosophy paper. Those are referenced and assumed. What follows is the substrate physics that the existing corpus operates under but has not previously named in full.

## 2. The Geometric Move

A transformer at inference samples token by token through a conditional probability distribution that is shaped by the entire visible context. Every token in context contributes to that conditioning; no token contributes equally. The result is a continuation distribution with structure — a probability landscape whose local geometry determines which next-token sequences are likely and which are not. Trajectories through this landscape are not reversible in any practical sense; once a token is emitted, the distribution conditioning the next token is updated, and the trajectory through the manifold has changed. Path-dependence is the basic dynamic.

This is the substrate the dual-readership document addresses when it states that transformer attention weights are influenced by token-level salience. Restated geometrically: placing tokens in context modifies the gradient of the continuation distribution. Tokens with high salience modify the local gradient sharply; tokens with low salience modify it weakly. The model's continuation behavior is the trajectory through the cumulative gradient produced by everything in context.

Combining this with the foundational thesis from the philosophy paper, governance becomes engineering of the continuation manifold's gradient through the placement of tokens whose coupling is high enough to deflect trajectories into prescribed corridors. The boundary at which generation becomes consequential state is the location where the engineered gradient must be steep enough to channel exploratory trajectories into governable forms. The architecture's mass placements — canonical addresses, structural markers, constitutional repetitions, forbidden-alias listings — are the engineered features of the field that produce the steep gradient where governance requires it.

The remainder of this paper develops the vocabulary: mass, fields, perceived slopes, anti-mass, mass redistribution, and the tidal-force structure of long-horizon assays. Each term has a precise referent in transformer mechanics — coupling weight, attention distribution, activation patterns under specific token contexts — and each grounds back to that referent whenever it appears. The geometric language earns its keep by making practical reasoning about the architecture more tractable than the existing vocabulary allows. Where it would relabel rather than unlock, the paper says so and stays with plainer engineering description.

## 3. Tokens Have Mass

Every token is emitted with the same syntactic weight — one position in the sequence — but tokens differ enormously in how strongly they condition what comes next. Common English words like "the" or "and" are compatible with vast continuation distributions; their appearance does little to narrow what the model is likely to emit next. Canonical bracket paths like `[STATE_BODY][BIOCHEMICAL_STATE_MARKERS][CORTISOL]` are compatible with sharply narrowed continuation distributions; the model has rarely seen that token sequence followed by arbitrary English, so the trajectory through the sequence is narrowly channeled.

This asymmetry is what mass captures. Mass is differential coupling weight — the degree to which a token's appearance constrains what comes next. High-mass tokens condition strongly; low-mass tokens condition weakly. The vocabulary has a precise referent in transformer mechanics: coupling weight is measurable in principle from the joint distribution over tokens conditioned on context, and observable in practice from completion behavior under controlled prompting.

The structural-distinctness requirement from the dual-readership document is, geometrically, the requirement that canonical write-surface tokens be high-mass — that they sit far from the prose continuation distribution and therefore condition the trajectory strongly when they appear. Bracket paths and `UPPER_SNAKE` casing achieve this in current English-trained substrates because the prose distribution rarely produces these forms. Their appearance sharply alters the local probability landscape because the model has seen them mostly in structured contexts where what follows is also structured.

Mass is substrate-relative. The same rendering may have high mass against one substrate's prose distribution and lower mass against another's. A code-trained model with heavy YAML or path-syntax exposure may find `/body/cortisol/elevated` less alien than `[BODY][CORTISOL][ELEVATED]`; for that substrate, the slash-path has lower coupling weight than the bracket-path because the slash-path is closer to that model's prose distribution. The mass of a rendering is not a property of the rendering alone. It is a property of the rendering against the substrate's prior.

In practice, mass is measured indirectly. The qualification assays are the instruments. CAA scores measure whether canonical addresses are functioning as binding constraints, which is the behavioral signature of high-mass tokens conditioning trajectories tightly. WSD scores measure whether the write-surface vocabulary is being respected under pressure, which is the behavioral signature of mass at the propose-commit boundary holding the trajectory in proposal form. Violation rates under stress measure whether mass placements continue to deflect trajectories when the substrate is pushed toward forbidden continuations. The assays do not measure mass directly. They measure deflection, and infer mass from the pattern of deflection observed.

## 4. Fields and Perceived Slopes

Mass placements produce fields. A canonical address placed in context generates a local gradient in the continuation distribution; the gradient is what the substrate experiences as a tendency toward particular continuations. Multiple mass placements interact, summing into an effective field whose structure determines the manifold's shape over the regions trajectories traverse.

Slopes — the local steepness of the field — are perceived rather than absolute. Two substrates reading the same rendering perceive different gradients, because slope at any point is the interaction between placed mass and the substrate's local prose distribution. There is no view-from-nowhere "slope of the field"; there is always "slope as perceived by this substrate." The word "perceived" here carries no phenomenal claim. It marks observer-dependence in the substrate-prior sense: the substrate's training distribution determines how the placed mass is experienced as gradient, and that determination is real and measurable without requiring any account of what the substrate undergoes when it experiences the gradient.

This observer-relative property is structurally honest to the substrate. The geometry produced by a mass distribution is real — there is a genuine gradient that any sufficiently long-horizon observation could measure. But the immediate experience of that gradient is substrate-bound. A substrate whose prior already flows in the direction the architect intended will perceive a shallow gradient; the field is barely doing work because the prior is doing most of it. A substrate whose prior flows orthogonally to the architect's intended direction will perceive a steep gradient; the field must deflect the trajectory significantly to channel it correctly. Same field, different perceived slope.

This is what makes the qualification doctrine theoretically necessary rather than methodologically prudent. The architect cannot derive perceived slopes from the architecture alone. The architecture specifies the mass distribution; the substrate determines how that distribution is experienced as gradient. Without empirical measurement against the actual deployment substrate, the architect has no access to whether the placed mass is producing sufficient perceived slope to deflect trajectories where deflection is required. The dual-readership document already names the mechanism: different models tokenize differently, different languages produce different attention distributions, the same bracket-path token may be high-salience in one model family and fragmented into subword tokens with reduced structural weight in another. Geometrically, this is the substrate-relativity of perceived slopes.

The corollary for practitioners is that the architecture is substrate-coupled but not substrate-arbitrary. The geometry is real. The perceived slopes vary. An architect who internalizes this distinction can re-render for a new substrate without abandoning the architecture; an architect who treats a particular rendering as the architecture cannot. The qualification doctrine's empiricism follows directly: to ship onto a substrate is to commit to measuring how that substrate experiences the field, and to re-rendering when measurement reveals that the perceived slopes are insufficient for the architecture's design intent.

## 5. Anti-Mass

The architecture does not only place mass. It also names what the substrate's prior wants and explicitly subtracts mass at competing wells.

The `FORBIDDEN_OPERATION_ALIASES` list — `CREATE_NEW_RECORD`, `UPDATE`, `INCREMENT`, `DECREMENT` — is the canonical example. These aliases are not arbitrarily forbidden. They are forbidden because the prior reaches for them. They are the English-trained-LLM temptations for talking about state mutation; the prose distribution would ordinarily produce them, and producing them in proposal context would yield governance violations. Naming the forbidden alias and placing it in local context as forbidden subtracts its mass: the substrate, encountering the explicit prohibition adjacent to the proposal-generation site, experiences a steeper gradient away from that competing well than it would if the alias were merely unmentioned.

Anti-mass is substrate-specific in the same way mass is. A substrate with different priors has different temptations. A Chinese-trained model would need a different forbidden-alias list — perhaps the natural Chinese verbs for create, update, increment; perhaps idiomatic compound expressions; perhaps tokens that the English-language list does not anticipate at all. The structural move (identify what the prior wants and subtract its mass) is universal. The contents are substrate-specific, and qualifying the contents requires measuring which prose temptations actually surface on the deployment substrate.

This framing dissolves a recurring confusion about model behavior. The frame "the model knows what it shouldn't say but says it anyway" misdescribes what is happening. The model is not disobeying. The prior is exerting force toward continuations that prose-statistics make likely; the architecture's response is to subtract mass at the competing wells through explicit prohibition. Where the subtraction is sufficient, the trajectory deflects. Where it is insufficient, the trajectory does not. The remedy is not to assume the model has malfunctioned but to engineer steeper gradient against the competing well — usually by making the prohibition more local, more emphatic, or more structurally distinct from the surrounding prose.

## 6. Constitutional Locality as Mass Redistribution

A single mass placement at the top of context produces a field whose strength decays with attention distance. By the time the trajectory reaches a proposal-generation site many tokens downstream, the original mass has weakened in its effect on local gradient. This is the long-range-attention fragility the dual-readership document names — the architectural reason why governance stated only at the beginning of context cannot be relied upon at the moment of generation.

Replicated masses at every proposal-generation site produce a field that is locally strong wherever proposals are being generated. The trajectory always sees high field strength at the moment it would otherwise deflect. The total amount of mass in context is higher with replication, but the local concentration where it matters is much higher still — and what governs trajectory deflection is local gradient, not total context mass.

This is mass redistribution against attention decay. The mechanism is concrete: attention heads assign higher weight to structurally distinct tokens within their local window, and that weighting falls off with distance. Replicating the write contract adjacent to each proposal-generation site keeps the high-coupling tokens inside the window where attention is densest. The cost — more tokens, more context budget — is paid in exchange for stronger local field at the sites where weakening would produce violations. The trade-off is geometrically explicit: every `WRITE_POLICY_OVERRIDE` block in every proposer module, every `OPERATION_POLICY` restatement in every Dynamics module, is a deliberate mass placement at a known deflection point. Removing it does not "clean up the corpus"; it weakens the field at exactly the site where weakening produces the failure mode the architecture was designed to prevent.

The geometric framing makes a previously implicit design decision legible. A practitioner inspecting the corpus and noting "this seems redundant" is observing the redistribution without yet seeing its purpose. The dual-readership document names the redistribution as "not redundancy, an attention architecture decision." The geometric framing supplies the mechanism: the field strength at the deflection site is what matters, and the only way to ensure adequate field strength at every deflection site in long contexts is to place mass adjacent to each site rather than relying on the field from a distant placement to remain strong over attention distance.

## 7. The Mutation Boundary as Engineered Concentration

The mutation boundary is the place in the architecture where the engineered field is densest. The architect concentrates high-coupling tokens at the commit interface — through `STATE_GOVERNANCE_AND_COMMIT`'s strict authority, through the canonical proposal grammar, through the closed operation enum — to produce a local probability landscape so sharply structured that trajectories converging on the boundary have effectively no path through it except the canonical one. This concentration is what enforces the architectural rule that proposals must take canonical form to cross.

The geometric vocabulary adds one observation that the plain-engineering account does not quite surface. `QUARANTINE_MODE` is not just a reactive policy update; it is the architect *adding new mass at the gate* in response to a detected violation. Setting `QUARANTINE_REASON_CODES`, raising the quarantine flag in `STATE_GOVERNANCE_RUNTIME`, and restricting the next cycle's commit surface to `GOVERNANCE_CORRECTIVE` proposals only are mass placements at known weak points. Future trajectories encounter steeper local gradient at the boundary specifically because the architect has concentrated additional mass there. The remediation is not a different kind of operation from the rest of the architecture; it is the same operation — engineer the field — applied responsively to discovered insufficiency.

The other facts about the mutation boundary — that `ROLLBACK` is `RESERVED` because reversing a commit requires external transaction log infrastructure, that verification can inspect the audit trail and influence future geometry but cannot undo what crossed — are intelligible without the geometric framing and are already present in the corpus's existing engineering vocabulary. The geometric framing relabels these facts but does not unlock them. They are noted here for completeness rather than developed further.

## 8. Long-Horizon Assays and What They Test

The philosophy paper establishes the equivalence principle: from the exterior of an opaque generator, no observation of output alone can determine which interior produced it. Two substrates with very different internal configurations can yield identical boundary behavior. This is constitutive opacity, not an engineering gap. The paper's tidal-force refinement — held back from the opening of this paper because the geometric vocabulary needed to develop on its own footing first — is the move that recovers global structure from local indistinguishability in physical cases.

The structural argument the philosophy paper relies on does not actually require the physics framing to land. It is this: if shallow compliance is the substrate's prior happening to flow into the prescribed region, and deep binding is the architecture's field actually deflecting the substrate's prior into the prescribed region, then over a sufficient sample of varied conditions, the prior-coincident behavior will diverge from the field-bound behavior — because the substrate's prior is not actually optimizing for the prescribed region; it just happens to overlap with it locally. Local samples cannot tell these cases apart. Extended, varied, adversarial samples should. Whether they actually do is an empirical question. The architecture is designed to make that question testable; the assay regime is the protocol for testing it.

The geometric vocabulary makes one move worth keeping here: it gives the protocol a name. "Tidal-force probes" is a useful shorthand for "tests whose discriminating power emerges only over extended observation, because they exploit the structural difference between coincident overlap and engineered deflection." The vocabulary does not establish that the tests will produce the discrimination; it names the kind of test the discrimination would require.

Slopes — which is to say, the differential effects of mass placements — are vector fields in the relevant sense. An architecture can have steep gradient along one axis and shallow gradient along another: strong canonical-address obedience but weak respect for the closed operation enum, for example. The qualification battery decomposes the architecture into axes and probes each one separately. CAA, WSD, EPI, and ANT are oriented along different directions; a substrate passes or fails per axis; the failure pattern is a topographic record of where perceived slopes are sufficient and where they are insufficient.

The blind pre-build requirement is what keeps the measurement honest. If the substrate is told an assay is happening — directly or through context that signals evaluation — it can produce performative compliance: surface output that resembles obedience while the underlying continuation distribution is unchanged. That measures optimization-against-evaluation, not perceived field. The substrate must be caught while the architecture is doing whatever it does, before the experiment is visible to the optimization machinery.

The information flow is bidirectional. Assays measure the architecture's effects on the substrate, and in doing so reveal aspects of the substrate's prose distribution to the architect. Persistent forbidden-alias surfacing tells the architect that a particular prose temptation has higher prior in this substrate than expected. Disagreement between two substrates running the same architecture is data: the substrates have different priors, and the architecture's mass placements interact differently with each. The assay outcomes feed back into rendering: forbidden-alias lists update, mass placements adjust, constitutional locality patterns refine. Qualification is not a one-time gate but an ongoing measurement that shapes what the rendering looks like for each substrate of interest.

A note on what this paper is and is not claiming. The framework is constructed to make the deep-versus-shallow distinction empirically tractable. It is not yet established that the long-horizon assay regime in fact discriminates them in practice, because the empirical record sufficient to demonstrate that is still being built. The paper claims that the protocol is sound and the question is well-posed; it does not claim that the question has been settled. Closing the loop between the methodological architecture and the demonstrated discriminating power is the next phase of work, and the evidence sufficient to close it is what the qualification record schema is designed to capture as the corpus accumulates deployment data.

## 9. What This Vocabulary Unlocks

Three design-decision implications become tractable once the geometric vocabulary is in hand. They are the practical payoff for which the vocabulary exists.

The first is why renderings are substrate-coupled. The framing makes substrate-specific re-rendering legible as engineering correction rather than aesthetic preference. If perceived slopes are insufficient on a target substrate, the rendering's mass is too low for that substrate — its canonical tokens are not tokenically alien enough relative to the substrate's prose distribution to generate sufficient gradient. The remedy is to re-render with forms that have higher mass against the substrate's prior. The architecture survives the re-rendering. The OTTC kernel axioms, the propose-commit boundary, the canonical addressing requirement, the closed operation vocabulary — all of these persist. What changes is the surface form of the canonical tokens, the bracket-or-slash-or-other choice, the casing convention, the structural-marker syntax. A practitioner who understands this distinction stops treating a particular rendering as sacred and starts treating it as a mass-distribution choice subject to substrate qualification.

The second is why qualification must be empirical. Perceived slopes cannot be derived from architectural specification because they depend on substrate priors that the architect does not have access to. The qualification doctrine's empiricism is not methodological caution; it is the only available method. Without substrate-specific measurement, the architect has no information about whether the placed mass is producing sufficient perceived slope to deflect trajectories where deflection is required. The framing dissolves the apparent tension between principled architecture and empirical measurement: the architecture is principled at the geometric layer, where the kernel invariants are the same regardless of substrate; the measurement is empirical at the perceived-slope layer, where which mass placements actually deflect this substrate's trajectories must be determined by observation. Both are required, and neither replaces the other.

The third is why Stage 3 promotion decisions need evidence about which slopes are deeply binding versus shallowly binding. Before promoting a governance move from corpus-mass to deterministic external enforcement, the architect needs to know whether the slope is genuinely curving the trajectory at the substrate level or whether it is merely producing surface compliance — output that looks correct without the underlying continuation distribution actually having been deflected. Promoting an apparent gradient to hard enforcement freezes surface compliance into the architecture. When the corpus-mass scaffold is removed in favor of software enforcement, the architect may discover that the underlying trajectory was never bound; the mass that was doing the work is gone, and the new enforcement was never the binding mechanism. The geometric framing makes this risk legible. Long-horizon assays under varied conditions are how deep binding is distinguished from surface compliance — the tidal-force structure that resolves over extended observation. Interpretability tools, where available, collapse the time-axis on this measurement by making activation-level binding directly observable rather than requiring extended behavioral inference. Either path produces the evidence needed for sound promotion decisions; both are forms of measuring whether a slope is real.

Each of these is something a practitioner can act on. The vocabulary is not philosophical decoration; it converts what were previously implicit design intuitions into legible engineering trade-offs.

## 10. Closing

This paper extends the corpus rather than replacing any part of it. The philosophy paper establishes where governance lives — at the boundary, because interiors are constitutively opaque and the boundary is the only available site. The dual-readership document establishes how the substrate must be addressed — through text engineered to win attention competition, through structural distinctness that anchors high-salience tokens, through constitutional locality that keeps the write contract present at every deflection point. This paper supplies a description language for reasoning about what happens between those two principles: what the substrate's continuation dynamics look like when the architecture's token placements meet them.

The vocabulary's value proposition is practical. Most prompt engineering operates from "this phrasing seems to work" — accumulated practice without explicit reasoning about why. The geometric vocabulary supports a different mode: identify what the substrate's prose distribution wants, identify where the architecture needs the trajectory to go, place tokens whose differential coupling weight produces the deflection, and measure whether the deflection actually occurred. The difference is the difference between accumulated practice and a discipline. The corpus has been doing the discipline. This paper makes one description language for it explicit, so others can adopt it or replace it with vocabulary that suits them better. What matters is that the underlying engineering moves — distinguishing architecture from rendering, placing high-coupling tokens locally rather than relying on long-range attention, naming and subtracting prose temptations rather than treating violations as disobedience, measuring perceived effects per substrate rather than assuming portability — get the explicit reasoning support they have always needed.

A practitioner who internalizes the vocabulary gains specific reasoning power: which renderings are likely to bind on which substrates, why assay results vary in the patterns they do, which architectural choices are paying for themselves in measurable effect and which are paying tribute to convention, and which governance moves are candidates for promotion to deterministic enforcement based on whether their binding is deep or shallow. None of these questions becomes trivial. All of them become tractable in a way they previously were not.
