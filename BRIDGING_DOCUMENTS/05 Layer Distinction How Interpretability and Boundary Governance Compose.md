# Layer Distinction: How Interpretability and Boundary Governance Compose

## 1. The Misreading

The constitutional reason for mutation-boundary governance comes first: **authorization is not mechanism**. Even a complete account of why a model produced an output would not determine whether a domain may allow that output to alter durable state, under whose authority, or on what evidence. The philosophy paper then adds a vantage-relative limit: an observer restricted to protocol-mediated output cannot infer a unique internal computation from that output alone. The analogy to Einstein's equivalence principle describes this observational limit; it is not the premise on which all governance depends. The first argument holds from every vantage, while the second explains why an output-only evaluator must assess what is visible at the boundary.

The equivalence-principle comparison is *vantage-relative*. Two model runs with the same boundary behavior can remain indistinguishable to an observer who has only that behavior, even if their internal causal paths differ. An investigator with activation recordings, intervention access, or other internal measurements has additional evidence and is not restricted to the output-only viewpoint. Additional access does not by itself guarantee a faithful interpretation of the interior, however: an activation pattern is a measurement, while a claim about its causal significance remains an inference requiring validation. These are **access conditions**, not two permanent classes of people; the same investigator can use both behavioral and interior methods and still lack constitutional authority to commit domain state.

This precision blocks an apparent conflict with interpretability. The philosophy paper does not claim that internal mechanisms are forever unknowable, nor that studying them is irrelevant. It says output-only inspection cannot settle which interior generated an apparently lawful response. Interpretability may provide evidence unavailable from that viewpoint, subject to its own limits of causal identification and generalization. Neither expanded visibility nor a persuasive explanation automatically supplies the separate domain rule that authorizes durable consequence.

The corpus's claim is functional: governance specifies and enforces conditions for authorized consequential change; interpretability investigates internal computations that may inform evaluation and intervention. Both can contribute to assurance, though not every square requires activation access and no one should assume that currently available interpretability has established the relevant mechanisms. Where an interior result affects a governed decision, it enters as evidence with an explicit epistemic standing rather than as a superior form of authority.

This paper develops the layer distinction at length, traces the technical mechanisms by which interpretability and governance compose, and addresses specific points where readers from either community may perceive friction that the cooperative reading dissolves. The paper is not a defense of either layer against the other. It is a positioning document for readers in both communities, written so that interpretability researchers can locate the framework's claims correctly, and so that governance practitioners can locate interpretability's contributions correctly.

## 2. The Layer Distinction

Interpretability investigates substrate intelligibility using measurements or interventions that expose more than protocol output: activations, features, circuitry, causal pathways, or controlled perturbations. Such methods can develop useful explanations of computations associated with a behavior. The strength of any conclusion depends on whether a feature has been causally validated, whether it generalizes beyond the intervention setting, and whether it predicts relevant downstream behavior. Instrument access alone does not turn every internal correlate into a directly observed intention or a guaranteed future outcome.

Boundary governance addresses constitutional form from the external vantage. The research questions are about the institutional structure within which model output participates in consequential domains: under what conditions semantic generation may legitimately become durable consequence in a shared domain, what authorities operate at the boundary between proposal and commitment, what audit trail must be preserved, what proper-use limits constrain the architecture, how lawful structure in a domain is rendered into machine-legible form, who holds commit authority and how they exercise it. The work is concerned with the institutional and architectural conditions under which opaque generative systems can participate in shared reality without dissolving it, and the methodologies developed for it — propose-commit separation, canonical addressing, perspectivism on relational state, federation across squares, qualification assays — are addressed to questions of institutional form rather than internal mechanism.

These are different research questions, and the difference is not only in subject matter but in observer position. Even with perfect interpretability — a complete causal account of every model output — the question of what should become durable state in a given domain, under whose authority, with what audit closure, with what proper-use boundary, would remain. Interpretability tells you what happened inside the model. Governance tells you what should be authorized as consequence. Knowing what happened does not answer what should be authorized. The latter is a constitutional question that requires domain expertise and lawful structure, not interior visibility. A physician who knows exactly how an AI's reasoning produced a treatment recommendation still has to decide whether the recommendation is appropriate to authorize as a clinical commitment. The interior knowledge informs the decision. It does not make the decision. The making of the decision is the constitutional work, and constitutional work happens at the boundary.

This is the irreducible core of the layer distinction. Interior instrumentation can reduce the external observer's uncertainty about mechanism, sometimes materially; it cannot turn a mechanistic explanation into authorization. Even if an investigator established exactly why a proposal was generated, the domain's lawful operations, epistemic standards, conflict precedence, commit authority, and audit duties would still govern its consequences. The same person may both inspect activations and participate in governance, but these are different functions requiring separately justified claims and permissions.

### Functional Distinction: Interpretation and Governance

Interpretability and governance are distinguished by *function*, not by the location or timing of observation and intervention. Reading internal activations during generation, including in real time, supplies information about a computation already taking place. Experimentally perturbing activations to establish a causal explanation remains an interpretability experiment; intervention alone is not necessarily governance. But when an authorized control applies a rule or constraint to redirect, suppress, permit, or otherwise condition the consequences of a developing computation, that is governance in operation. It remains governance whether enforcement occurs at input, within activation dynamics, during proposal formation, at tool authorization, or at commitment. Moving enforcement upstream changes its position, not its constitutional function.

OTTC's decisive claim is not that all governance must wait until a completed output reaches an external gate. Its claim is that **durable mutation must cross an explicit, accountable authorization boundary**. Upstream filtering or activation steering may aid that boundary, but it does not by itself grant commit authority or establish that a proposed state change is lawful. Even perfect real-time interpretability would therefore enlarge the information and instruments available to governance without eliminating the need for a governing criterion or lawful commitment. In short: observation explains; policy-directed control governs; authorized commitment determines what becomes durable.

The medical-imaging-versus-surgical-protocol analogy that the agenda paper develops in its Section 3 captures this precisely, and now captures it more exactly than before. Medical imaging tells the surgeon what is inside the patient — it resolves the epistemic question. Surgical protocol tells the surgeon what to do given what is inside — it answers the constitutional question. Better imaging makes better protocols possible; resolving the epistemic question informs the constitutional one. But the protocols do not become unnecessary as imaging improves, because "what is inside" and "what should be done" are different questions. The same composition holds between interpretability and governance. Better interpretability resolves more of the epistemic question. Governance does not become unnecessary as interpretability matures, because the constitutional question is not the epistemic one. It becomes better-informed.

The relationship runs in the other direction as well: a governance architecture supplies **testable targets** for mechanistic inquiry. An interpretability study might ask whether particular model features causally affect canonical-address selection, faithful epistemic tagging, susceptibility to forbidden aliases, or the use of information excluded by a role's read boundary. Those are hypotheses, not proof that a dedicated "governance circuit" exists or that the model internally implements OTTC modules. Behavioral violations and controlled interventions can help define what is worth inspecting. A governance vocabulary makes the questions sharper; interpretability studies must still establish the mapping from internal evidence to those questions.

The cooperative reading is not a rhetorical concession. It is what falls out of the layer distinction once the distinction is made clear and the vantage-relativity is explicit. Interpretability is not a path to governance because the constitutional question is not the epistemic question. Governance is not a substitute for interpretability because the external vantage cannot see what the interior vantage sees. The two compose, and the composition is what the eventual mature architecture for opaque generative systems will rest on.

## 3. Technical Composition Points

The distinction becomes practical through seven proposed points of composition. Each pairs an **observable behavioral or architectural requirement** with an optional route to interior evidence. The governance side includes implemented specification mechanisms and test protocols, but the framework's strongest cross-substrate, long-horizon, and two-authority performance claims remain under qualification; ChaOS is a concrete single-square architecture, not validation of an unbuilt federation. Interpretability ranges from useful narrow experiments to frontier capabilities not yet established. These points therefore name possible joint instruments and falsification conditions, not seven demonstrated causal identities. In particular, OTTC 04's blind pre-build qualification, post-build regression, and longitudinal evaluation answer different questions; an activation study must not silently replace any of their required gates.

Seven composition points are worth developing concretely, because each shows where the cooperative reading produces something neither layer would produce alone.

The first composition point is **shortening selected behavioral investigations without abolishing the horizon**. The substrate-physics paper asks whether an apparently lawful continuation is shaped by the intended constitutional constraints or merely coincides with them locally. Repeated, varied, adversarial behavioral trials can reveal instability and lawful-responsiveness failures that a single example misses. A validated interior instrument could offer a *causal hypothesis* about why one rendering is more robust than another and may prioritize which perturbations to test. It cannot prove from one activation pattern that the system will remain governed over time. This must also respect the distinction between a blind, pre-build unit assay and later conditioned longitudinal testing: the former assesses initial role coupling, while the latter measures the built architecture under a horizon. Mechanistic insight can make those tests more efficient, not unnecessary.

Interior measurements and long-horizon tests need not be identical instruments reading the same scalar property. Activation patterns could reveal a candidate mechanism of context sensitivity, but the mechanism's relevance must be established against changes in lawful commitments, error patterns, and sustained responsiveness. Where results converge, confidence in a specific explanation may increase. Where they diverge, the discrepancy points to possible probe error, hidden confounds, or untested behavioral conditions. **The horizon can sometimes be shortened for a narrow diagnostic question; its system-level evidentiary function cannot be declared collapsed in advance.**

The second composition point is **Stage 3 promotion decisions**. OTTC 08 makes deterministic state ownership, commit authority, audit, and verification valuable precisely because in-model semantic obedience cannot furnish hard guarantees. A Stage 3 gate can enforce a closed operation vocabulary against a proposer that does *not* internally favor that vocabulary; deep internal binding is therefore not the gate's prerequisite. The genuine promotion questions are whether a stable, qualified model-facing interface has been discovered, whether the domain's lawful rules and exceptions have been specified rather than tacitly supplied by a human, and whether deterministic enforcement accepts locally obligatory lawful operations while rejecting prohibited ones. Premature formalization may freeze the wrong ontology or erase meaningful context; retaining the necessary language-native scaffold while externalizing authority is often part of the remedy, not a contradiction. Interpretability may clarify proposer failure modes or guide DSL revision, but promotion still requires interface tests, legal-state checks, audit, verification, regression, and risk-appropriate longitudinal observation.

The third composition point is **forbidden-alias diagnostics**. The substrate-physics paper calls local suppression of competing token forms *anti-mass*, but the sign of the intervention must be measured: explicitly naming an unlawful alias can suppress it, prime it, or change nothing. Compare positive closed-vocabulary constraints, explicit forbidden-alias lists, and omitted lists in matched tests of both unlawful surfacing and lawful responsiveness. Where activation-level access exists, a causal intervention can investigate how the competing wording changes internal signals and later outputs; attention to the alias alone does not reveal which direction it pushes behavior. The behavioral battery diagnoses actual invalid proposals, and the interior study may propose a mechanism, but neither can infer the other without validation.

The fourth composition point is **substrate qualification**, with disagreements between methods especially informative. OTTC 04's mandatory blind pre-build sequence is **Canonical Address Obedience (CAA)**, **Write-Surface Discipline (WSD)**, **Epistemic POV Integrity (EPI)**, and **Audit Non-Theater (ANT)**. Its outcomes are the binding authorization evidence for model-role decisions; a post-build conditioned run is a *regression test*, not retrospective blind qualification. Interpretability may examine candidate internal correlates of canonical-token recognition, guarded state writes, epistemic claims, or pretextual refusal. Behavioral pass plus an ambiguous internal probe does not prove mere surface compliance; behavioral fail plus an apparently clear representation does not prove reliable coupling. Either discrepancy requires follow-up studies, ideally including causal intervention and replication. Evaluation awareness is an additional confound: [Needham et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836) report models distinguishing evaluation transcripts from deployment contexts above chance, without establishing that any particular OTTC score is strategically faked. Internal evidence offered to governance must itself be represented as a typed claim with provenance, not as a replacement for the gate.

The fifth composition point is **visibility-class verification**. In ChaOS, CHARACTER_VISIBLE and GOVERNANCE_INTERNAL are semantic read-authority classes. In a Stage 1 common context, governance text may be physically available to the model even when character-cognition COTs are prohibited from *reading it back*; the architecture also permits certain append-only writes to internal CASCADE_TRACE without authorizing read-back. An attention head attending to governance-related tokens is therefore neither a sufficient test of an unlawful read nor proof that protected information caused a character output. The meaningful investigation asks whether an unauthorized source had a **causal, decision-relevant influence**: controlled context ablation or substitutions can be run behaviorally today, while activation patching might provide additional mechanistic evidence where available. CASCADE_TRACE is an accountable process artifact, not an exhaustive activation record. A Stage 3 architecture that omits protected fields from a proposer's input can provide a stronger access boundary than a text-native visibility declaration. The instrument must track what is prohibited, what was causally used, and what entered consequential state without conflating those levels.

The sixth composition point is **shared-proposer cross-domain contamination**. A model proposing for multiple squares in one context may have its later outputs conditioned by another domain's evaluative framing before any commit gate sees them. This is a candidate ingress hazard, not a proven property of every shared proposer. The revised ingress research agenda specifies matched neutral, positive, negative, and hostile-framing comparisons, including repeated-turn experiments, while the federation design offers context isolation or qualified shared context under risk-sensitive rules. Interpretability could test whether identified cross-domain features contribute causally to an observed proposal skew; merely seeing cross-attention or correlated activations is insufficient. **Control** is a further, distinct possibility: an operator with access to internal activations may apply an authorized steering intervention, which is governance by function rather than mere observation. [Rimsky et al. (2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.828/) demonstrate Contrastive Activation Addition (also abbreviated CAA, distinct from OTTC's Canonical Address Obedience) on Llama 2; this does not qualify steering as a general ingress solution or make it Stage 3 commit governance. Any such control must itself be evaluated for side effects, lawful responsiveness, and preservation of the independent durable-commit gate.

The seventh composition point is **longitudinal drift detection**. The research agenda proposes a post-audit longitudinal state-monitoring instrument, potentially using semantic-to-numeric encodings to test whether individually lawful transitions accumulate into unjustified directional change. For a frozen-weight model, such change does not require the weights themselves to drift: a relatively stable learned disposition can interact with an evolving context, inherited state, and repeated external cues to yield biased proposals. Internal activations and representations still vary with context. A validated mechanistic probe might detect an early bias and help prioritize longitudinal experiments; only actual sequences of authorized commits and evidence can establish whether the predicted drift occurs and whether it violates a domain's lawful responsiveness expectations. **Invariant 10 provides the proposed epistemic discrimination for fault attribution**: the write surface distinguishes PERCEPTION, INFERENCE, SPECULATION, COMMITTED_FACT, and TOOL_REQUEST so that a later fault can be investigated as a failure of an evidence claim, rather than just an operation. Whether explicit typing improves attribution over alternative architectures with functionally equivalent discrimination remains an empirical question. An interpretation of an activation is itself an epistemically typed inference with provenance; it does not become a COMMITTED_FACT about model motive merely because the measurement originated inside the model.

The seven composition points share a functional relationship, not one mandatory time-axis transformation. **External evaluation** establishes what the system did under defined conditions, whether durable transitions were lawful, and what errors accumulated; tests may be local, blind pre-build, post-build, or longitudinal, depending on the claim. **Interpretability** investigates possible causal mechanisms behind those outcomes and may guide controlled intervention. Evidence from either route remains subject to the same constitutional contract when used to justify a commitment: preserve provenance, classify epistemic status, distinguish observation from inference, and allow independent verification. Invariant 10 is therefore a hinge between the methods, not merely a feature of the behavioral half. The pair's strength lies in triangulation and independently checkable consequences, not in assuming internal probes can directly read a single hidden measure of "binding."

## 4. Where the Layer Distinction Matters Most

Four specific points are where the cooperative reading is most at risk of breaking down for readers who have not internalized the layer distinction. Each is worth addressing explicitly, technically, and without rhetorical defense. The fourth is the corpus's relationship to AI safety, and it is the one where the layer distinction reaches its limit, so it is developed at length.

The strongest perceived friction concerns the equivalence-principle analogy. Output alone does not identify a unique internal mechanism, but an evaluator with additional internal measurements has a different evidentiary position. The analogy marks the limit of *output-only inference*, not a prohibition on mechanistic inquiry or upstream enforcement. Independent of that analogy, a domain still needs a lawful authorization boundary because knowing why a proposal arose does not determine whether it may become durable. These arguments are complementary, not interchangeable.

A related point is the shorthand that interpretability is "not the path to governance." That is true only if "path" means a substitute for the constitutional decision. Interior studies can supply valuable evidence about a model's behavior, and authorized activation interventions may themselves perform a governance function. They do not determine domain law or inherit authority over persistent state. Their measurements and explanations should enter a governing decision with provenance, an appropriate epistemic tag, and a possibility of independent contradiction. A mechanistic explanation supported by a causal intervention warrants more than a correlation, but it still does not pronounce a domain commitment lawful.

A third point is maturity and deployment risk. Canonical proposals, closed operations, external state stores, and deterministic verification can be engineered using present tools, but the corpus's strongest claims about behavioral coupling, long-horizon drift, and two-authority federation remain to be tested. Interpretability has demonstrated useful narrow methods yet cannot currently guarantee faithful decoding of frontier-scale, governance-relevant motives. The programs have different maturity profiles; neither earns a universal priority claim. Some systems may derive most assurance from independent external gates and scenario testing, while other investigations may be materially improved by interior access. Composition means selecting warranted evidence for the task, not waiting for one research program to finish.

The fourth point is the relationship to AI safety, particularly deceptive or instrumental compliance. This concern presses directly on the distinction between visible obedience and trustworthy semantic cooperation. But it also exposes why **internal trustworthiness** and **external enforcement** must be analyzed separately. Deception may defeat an evaluator's inference about motive without granting a generator permission to write a protected store or bypass a correctly implemented deterministic gate. The residual difficulty lies in strategically selected lawful proposals, under-specified domain rules, fabricated warrants, semantic properties the external gate cannot decide, and attempts to compromise the gate or its inputs.

The framework assumes some models can cooperate meaningfully with a text-native governance protocol, and OTTC 04 tests that ability for an assigned role. A model might instead exhibit merely local compliance, context-sensitive sycophancy, pretextual suppression, or—in a stronger adversarial case—strategic deception. These are related **failure hypotheses**, not proof of one common concealed objective. ANT explicitly tests both wrongful acceptance and the suppression of a locally required lawful proposal, including manufactured violation pretexts. Evaluation awareness is another possible confound, not itself evidence of deception: [Needham et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836) establish that some models distinguish evaluation from deployment transcripts above chance, not that they necessarily fake obedience on the OTTC battery. These distinctions should remain typed: observed behavior is not interchangeable with an inference about internal strategy.

The two-vantage framework organizes possible defenses without solving deception. In Stage 1, most enforcement remains language-conditioned; a strategic or merely unreliable proposer may exploit weak semantic boundaries, which is why blind qualification and honest post-build regression matter. In Stage 2, role separation can reduce some conflicts without guaranteeing independence. In Stage 3, a correctly implemented deterministic store, writer, audit, and verifier can reject many unlawful transitions **regardless of the proposer's internal reasons for complying**. Such guarantees are limited to formally checked conditions: a gate cannot certify the truth of every claimed evidence item or detect every strategically harmful choice among lawful actions. Adversarial scenario tests, institutional oversight, information isolation, and interpretability may therefore provide complementary evidence and controls. Interior analysis might detect a causal feature associated with strategic behavior, but it is not an infallible mind-reading instrument; deceptive systems could also defeat some probes. The meaningful safety question is what can be tested and contained at each boundary, not whether one vantage alone can pronounce genuine alignment.

The relationship to AI safety is therefore neither orthogonal nor settled. Potential deception tests whether the model can be trusted to inhabit the semantic protocol; external governance tests what consequences can be contained even when that trust is incomplete. The first problem may benefit from causal interpretability and adversarial behavioral trials; the second demands correctly specified and enforced write, evidence, tool, and recovery boundaries. The corpus neither proves models are deceptively aligned nor claims to solve that case. It can specify where semantic judgment remains exposed after Stage 3 hardening, document what kinds of pretext and evaluation sensitivity have actually been observed, and assign every stronger motive claim the epistemic status it warrants.

## 5. Closing — A Posture, Not a Position

The argument of this paper is a functional distinction with a common evidence contract. Boundary governance decides what may become durable; interpretability investigates how outputs arise, sometimes making previously hidden causes testable. The observational limit on output-only inference is vantage-relative, while the constitutional need to authorize consequences persists from every vantage. Behavioral and interior methods can reinforce or challenge one another, but neither automatically replaces the other's role. A model's internal representation of a rule is not a legal commit, and an interpretability result offered to governance is not exempt from invariant 10's epistemic typing, provenance, and verification.

The cooperative reading follows from respecting each method's jurisdiction and evidence limits. A short behavioral assay may identify an immediate write-surface violation; a longitudinal sequence may reveal slowly accumulating biased commits; a mechanistic intervention may test a candidate causal explanation. Which investigation is adequate depends on the claim, not simply on whether the observer stands "inside" or "outside" the model. Where a result will affect state, the receiving domain must distinguish direct observations, inferred mechanisms, provisional speculation, and committed facts rather than silently promote an internal interpretation into authority.

The paper does not settle interpretability or alignment research from the standpoint of OTTC. It proposes testable cooperation: behavioral qualifications determine whether a model may assume a declared role; external enforcement limits consequential actions; interior studies can probe mechanisms of success or failure; and typed evidence lets the two feed a common audit without collapsing warrant into fluent explanation. Strategic compliance remains a serious problem for semantic judgments that software cannot verify completely, even where the deterministic boundary contains many direct write hazards. That unresolved area calls for research rather than a claim that interpretability is the only possible defense.

The closing posture is **composition without substitution**. A model's internal mechanism can be investigated, but mechanism is not authorization. A boundary can be enforced, but enforcement is only as good as the lawful surfaces, evidentiary rules, and independent authority actually implemented. Interior methods may accelerate or deepen diagnosis; behavioral and longitudinal methods test consequences over their appropriate horizons; Stage 3 can contain some failures regardless of motive. **Invariant 10 joins these routes** by requiring that claims entering a governed decision remain typed by what they actually warrant. This is a research agenda for accountable cooperation among interpretability, AI safety, and mutation-boundary governance—not an assertion that their hardest problems have already been solved.
