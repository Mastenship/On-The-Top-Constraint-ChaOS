# Federated Governance and the Multi-Disciplinary Cognitive Substrate

## 1. The Coordination Problem

The hardest problems facing consequence-bearing fields right now are not single-domain problems. Climate-and-economy interactions. Pandemic preparedness across health-policy-economic-social-psychological dimensions. Long-term care systems that must integrate medical, legal, financial, family-relational, and ethical structures. Environmental remediation that must satisfy ecological, legal, economic, and community-justice constraints. These are problems that have resisted disciplinary attack for decades.

They fail not because we lack data, compute, or individual expertise. They fail because the integration surface does not exist. Domain experts cannot stay coherent across each other's terms long enough to produce hypotheses that all of them can stand behind. Every cross-domain conversation has to start from scratch on terminology, evidence standards, and what counts as a legitimate move. Last year's negotiated translation between epidemiology and economics is not load-bearing this year because there is no persistent constitutional substrate for multi-disciplinary cognition. The integration work is done freshly each time, by ad hoc teams, under deadlines that do not allow the patient negotiation cross-domain coordination actually requires.

OTTC's federation pattern is the structural answer. The canonical dictionary is the interlingua. The proposal grammar is the universal grammar of state-mutation speech-acts. Each square is sovereign in its own domain, governed by its own commit authority under its own lawful structure. Inter-square boundaries are governed surfaces with deterministic translation. Audit closure is preserved across the federation. The same architecture that governs single-domain mutation governs cross-domain coordination, because the same problem appears at both layers — opaque generators producing proposals that must become durable consequence under shared protocol.

The promise is not that federation solves the hard problems. The promise is that federation makes the integration surfaces exist for the first time at scale, so that domain experts working together can make cumulative progress on hard problems instead of starting over each time. This paper develops the federation pattern concretely. It uses a worked example at non-scientific register to demonstrate that the pattern is structural rather than speculative, and then shows how the same pattern operates at higher-stakes registers where the stakes motivate the federation work in the first place.

## 2. ChaOS and a Hypothetical WorldState — The Body-Mind Pair

ChaOS is the corpus's worked example of a single square. It governs the interior of an opaque agent: the body-mind, character cognition, the inner state surfaces of one entity moving through a world. Its commit authority is the human running the character, typically a tabletop game master. Its proper-use boundary is explicit: it is not a scientific instrument, it is not a model of human psychology, its psychological constructs function as semantically rich narrative control surfaces rather than as empirical synthesis. The construction history is developed directly in [Producing a Square: A Method Demonstration](./02%20Producing%20a%20Square%20A%20Method%20Demonstration.md): ChaOS preceded OTTC, and OTTC's candidate general invariants were extracted from the developing square before the two were refined in parallel. That account is the appropriate source for the history; this paper addresses the distinct question of federation.

A complete narrative simulation, in the structural sense the corpus uses for "narrative," requires more than a governed character. It requires the exterior — the world the character moves through, with its own lawful transitions independent of any single character's influence. Non-player characters following schedules. Map state evolving over time as buildings age, weather changes, terrain is altered. Faction dispositions shifting based on events the character did not witness. Seasons, economies, political pressures playing out at scales the character cannot directly perceive. None of this can be governed inside ChaOS, because ChaOS's domain is character cognition. A square that governs world state requires its own ontology, its own lawful transitions, its own commit authority.

This paper introduces a hypothetical companion square, WorldState, that would govern that exterior. WorldState is not built; it is sketched here only as much as the federation translation examples require. A full WorldState specification would be a separate document and would require its own constitutional discovery work. What this paper develops is the federation between ChaOS and a plausibly-shaped WorldState, using just enough of the hypothetical architecture to make the federation pattern concrete.

A methodological point worth surfacing before the federation work begins, and worth stating with more precision than the intuitive case usually receives: tabletop game masters have been running the *mechanics* of this federation intuitively for as long as tabletop games have existed. The DM tracks character state on character sheets and world state on maps, NPC schedules, faction trackers, calendars. The boundary between the two has been operationally managed by every competent DM for decades, with the DM mediating cross-boundary updates by judgment — the character's action affects the world; the world's evolution affects the character; the DM commits both sides and renders the result for the table.

It is important to be exact about what this precedent illustrates and what it does not, because the distinction bears directly on which parts of the federation pattern are demonstrated and which remain untested. Federation in the strict sense this paper develops requires two *separate sovereign commit authorities*, one per square, with negotiated, governed translation between them, and with the defining property that neither side holds authority over the other's commits. The tabletop DM is not two sovereigns. The DM is a single commit authority holding both ledgers, reconciling cross-boundary updates inside one head by judgment. This is not a defect of the tabletop case; it is the structure of the tabletop case, and it means the precedent is best understood as a *pre-federation* rather than a federation proper. It supplies an extensive human-practice precedent for three-layer rendering, lazy resolution, and perspectivist boundaries. It does not independently qualify an OTTC-governed translation mechanism, still less validate the axis that makes federation genuinely hard: two distinct authorities who can disagree, whose terms and permissions must be negotiated precisely because neither controls both sides. The DM does not have to negotiate a canonical-dictionary entry with an independent authority, because the DM holds both roles.

WorldState, if built and held by a *different* commit authority than ChaOS, would provide a first substantive test of the sovereign-separation axis. The example here is a proposed rendering, not a functioning two-square implementation. Human tabletop practice gives its boundary concepts a strong precedent under unified judgment, but no qualification results establish deterministic cross-square translation, lawful admissibility, or multi-authority reconciliation in an OTTC federation. The honest claim is that the mechanics are motivated and specified at a conceptual level while implementation, qualification, and the first genuinely two-authority test remain ahead. The higher-stakes paradigm cases in Section 6 likewise remain illustrative rather than built instances.

The pair has a structural property that makes it particularly suited as a federation example even in its pre-federation form. ChaOS governs the interior of an opaque agent — its body, mind, beliefs, perceptions, predictive frames, attributions. WorldState would govern the exterior the agent moves through. These are not two narrative squares that happen to be next to each other. They are complementary halves of a complete narrative simulation, each with its own lawful structure, and a real boundary between them that requires governance to cross. The federation work is rich precisely because the boundary is bidirectional, asymmetric, and consequential: character actions propose changes to world state; world evolution proposes inputs to character perception; each side has authority over its own commits; neither side has authority over the other's. The pair is what federation looks like when the two squares are genuinely distinct and genuinely interacting — and when the two commit authorities are also genuinely distinct, it is what the first true test of sovereign separation will look like.

## 3. Perspectivism as a Federation Design Decision

The naive design of a world-state square would have objective faction dispositions: Faction A is hostile to Faction B as a global fact. Disposition is a property of the world; the world has it; the character either knows it or does not. Most game-design state-tracking systems work this way, and most AI-world-modeling work conceives of world state along these lines.

A perspectivist design has faction dispositions as relational state surfaces that exist per perceiver. The character has their belief about Faction A's disposition toward Faction B. Faction A has its actual disposition toward Faction B. Faction B has its perception of Faction A's disposition. Other NPCs have their perceptions. Each is a separate state record. They may agree, may disagree, may be partially informed, may be deliberately deceived. Informational asymmetry is a first-class structural feature of the architecture, not an accident or a special case to be patched.

This is structurally identical to how ChaOS already handles relational state at the character-cognition layer. ChaOS does not store "the character's relationships" as a single objective table. It stores the character's beliefs about their relationships, which are subjective, partial, and revisable as the character learns. Other characters in ChaOS have their own relational beliefs about the same relationships, and the divergence between those beliefs is where most of the dramatic interest lives. Thoroughgoing perspectivism on relational state is one of the architectural moves that distinguishes ChaOS from simpler character-tracking systems. WorldState, in the design this paper sketches, would apply the same insight at the world-tracking layer, extended to factions, NPCs, and any other multi-agent relational structure that the world contains.

The implications for federation are significant. The canonical dictionary entry for "Faction A's disposition toward Faction B" cannot be a single field. It is a family of fields, one per perceiver: `[FACTION_DISPOSITION][PERCEIVER_ID][TARGET_ID]`. ChaOS proposing a change to the character's belief about Faction A translates as an update to the perceiver-equals-character entry, with no implication that any other perceiver's belief has changed. WorldState proposing a change to a faction's actual disposition — based on, say, an NPC's action that the character did not witness — updates the perceiver-equals-faction-self entry without touching the character's belief. The character's belief becomes outdated, and remains outdated until something causes it to update. The federation handles this informational asymmetry as a first-class structural feature: the boundary between squares carries multi-perceiver state cleanly, and the divergence between perceivers is not a problem to be resolved but a feature to be preserved.

The same perspectivism extends to map state, with a refinement that becomes architecturally important in the next section. When the character changes a location — burns down a tavern, builds a shrine, leaves graffiti — that change is committed in WorldState's map-state surface. But the location may continue to evolve under WorldState's lawful transitions independent of the character: the tavern is rebuilt over months by local townspeople, the shrine weathers, the graffiti is painted over. These evolutions do not propagate to the character's belief about the location until the character returns and perception forces reconciliation. The character's belief is stable; the world's actual state is stable; the gap between them is real and may persist for cycles or sessions until something closes it.

The same perspectivism extends to NPC state. The character believes an NPC is at one location with one disposition; the NPC actually is somewhere else with a different disposition; the gap closes only when the character perceives the NPC again — through direct encounter, through report from another character, through some indirect evidence that triggers a belief update. The federation handles this informational asymmetry cleanly because both sides have native perspectivism in their state surfaces. ChaOS knows it stores beliefs, not facts. WorldState knows it stores per-perceiver records, not omniscient state. The boundary between them carries the perspectivism through deterministic translation rather than collapsing it.

A recursive structural property is worth naming explicitly. The same relational-subjectivity pattern operates at every layer of the architecture: character belief, NPC belief, faction self-perception, world authoritative record. Thoroughgoing perspectivism is applied uniformly. This generalizes the architecture in a way that single-perspective world-tracking systems cannot match, and it generalizes upward to higher-stakes federation cases where informational asymmetry between domains is itself the load-bearing condition. Clinical state and legal state are not the same record with different access; they are constituted differently in each domain. Scientific evidence and policy evidence are not the same evidence at different audiences; they are evaluated under different standards by their respective authorities. The perspectivist design at the narrative layer is what allows the federation pattern to handle these higher-stakes cases without distortion, because the architectural commitment to per-perceiver state surfaces is established at the layer where it can be validated cheaply and extended where it cannot.

### 3.1 Designed Divergence Versus Federation-Layer Drift

Perspectivism deliberately designs divergence into the federation. The character's belief about the tavern stays stale until perception forces reconciliation; the gap between belief and actual state is legitimate and may persist for many cycles. This is a feature. But it introduces a measurement problem at the federation layer that mirrors, one level up, the longitudinal drift problem the agenda paper develops at the single-square layer in its Section 2.1 and Section 7.

Each square can be internally well-governed — every commit lawful, every transition audited — while its *boundary* mishandles an authorized information exchange. But an accumulating difference between a character's belief and the world's actual state is not itself decoherence. The character may never return to a town; an off-screen faction may change lawfully; the world may be represented by a lazy resolution rule until a visit actually demands a committed value. Even after exposure, belief need not converge immediately with world state: limited perception, ambiguous testimony, lawful confidentiality, and evidence-sensitive uncertainty can all warrant continued disagreement. An instrument that counts widening gaps alone as failures would misclassify intended perspectivism as instability.

The discriminator must therefore be conditioned on *lawful reconciliation opportunities*, not on the raw magnitude or direction of an unconsulted gap. For each configured inter-square boundary, the domain authorities must first specify which events trigger consultation; which information is observable or admissible; what the receiving square is obligated, permitted, or forbidden to update; and what counts as lawful deferral. At each triggered opportunity, inspect whether the source was resolved when required, whether disclosure and translation obeyed the boundary rules, whether the destination processed the evidence under its own authority, and whether every acceptance, deferral, or refusal had accountable closure. A persistent gap can pass this test when the receiving perspective lawfully retains uncertainty; a small gap can fail it when an authorized transfer is silently skipped or an unauthorized fact is leaked. The relevant longitudinal measures are missed or unlawfully handled opportunities, unjustified update failures, information leaks, and residual discrepancies *relative to the reconciliation duties actually triggered*. Gap trends remain useful descriptive context, not the definition of federation thermalization. Metrics, expected outcomes, and thresholds require definition before trials, and these are proposed instruments rather than measured results.

## 4. Lazy Evaluation and Proposer Sharing

Two specific patterns proposed for a ChaOS–WorldState pair generalize as design candidates beyond the narrative example. Tabletop practice illustrates how the underlying human mediation works; the corresponding governed two-square federation remains to be built and tested.

**Lazy evaluation.** The world is not simulated continuously between character visits. It is committed at the boundary moments where it matters — when the character would perceive it. Between visits, the world's actual state at a location exists only as a resolution rule plus an elapsed-time pointer. The inn was burned at cycle X; three months have elapsed; the resolution rule says rebuilding takes manpower and resources to estimate; an LLM operating as a proposer ruminates on the local manpower, available resources, and expected timeline; the proposer surfaces a candidate state — "the inn should be at 75% completion after three months"; the DM commits or adjusts that proposal as WorldState's commit authority; the committed state translates to ChaOS as perception input the next time the character returns; ChaOS processes the input through its Spatial COT and generates whatever attributions, modifiers, predictions, and impulse responses follow; the DM renders the resulting cross-square state into narrative for the table.

This is doing something architecturally specific. The world's actual state is committed only at boundary moments where it matters — not continuously simulated. The lazy-evaluation pattern keeps the architecture tractable by avoiding the simulation cost while preserving the perspectivism: the character's belief is whatever it last was; the world's actual state is committed only when forced; the resolution rule plus time delta is enough information to reconstruct current state when reconstruction is needed.

The pattern generalizes. In a clinical-and-research federation, intermediate research findings do not need to propagate continuously into clinical state until the clinical context forces reconciliation. In a legal-and-economic federation, regulatory changes do not need to update every economic model in real time; they update at the boundary moments where economic decisions actually consult the legal state. Lazy evaluation at federation boundaries is a general resource for keeping multi-square architectures tractable when the squares operate at different temporal cadences and cross-boundary updates are expensive.

**Proposer sharing.** The same LLM in the same conversation can be operating as proposer for both squares simultaneously — proposing character-side responses for ChaOS, proposing world-side computations for WorldState. The commit authorities remain separate, because what the character believes is committed under ChaOS's authority and what the world actually is is committed under WorldState's authority. The same proposer can serve multiple squares at the *commit* layer cleanly: a clinical-flavored proposal that reaches the legal square's commit gate can be rejected by the legal commit authority, because commit authority inspects and adjudicates what it is handed.

There is, however, a contamination channel that proposer sharing introduces and that commit-layer separation does not close, and it must be named precisely because an earlier and looser version of this claim would assert more protection than the architecture actually provides. Commit-layer separation protects against contamination at the *commit* surface. It does nothing about contamination at the *proposal and context* surface. If one proposer in one shared context window generates both clinical proposals and legal proposals, the clinical framing is present in the context, conditioning the legal proposal *distribution*, before any legal proposal reaches the attorney's commit gate. The attorney can reject a clinical-flavored legal proposal they can see. They cannot detect that their proposal distribution was subtly skewed by the adjacent domain's framing, because each generation is conditioned by the accumulated context, including the other domain's material, and the skew is in what was proposed rather than in any individual proposal's visible content.

This is structurally the same as the ingress-contamination mechanism proposed in the agenda paper's [Section 8](../The%20Mutation%20Boundary%20Problem%20-%20A%20Research%20Agenda%20for%20Stateful%20Opaque%20Generative%20Systems/8%20The%20Ingress%20Boundary.md): imported framing can condition evaluation before its evidentiary standing has been lawfully settled, and repeated turns may amplify it. Section 8 does *not* establish that all in-model ingress governance fails. It proposes matched-framing qualification and a risk-sensitive preference for early external separation when independence cannot be demonstrated, especially under high stakes. Commit-layer separation remains insufficient by itself: the earlier intuition that "the federation keeps proposals from contaminating each other's authority surfaces" is true only of lawfully adjudicated *commits*, not of the shared proposal distribution. A federation with a shared context can be commit-clean and proposal-exposed; the magnitude and manageability of the exposure have to be tested.

Two design responses are available, and they trade off against each other. The first is per-square context isolation: a deterministic intake gate presents each proposer invocation with only that square's authorized material, preventing direct shared-window exposure while sacrificing some of the efficiency of a unified context. Isolation controls this specified channel; it does not by itself guarantee unbiased evaluation or lawful downstream admission. The second is shared context with an explicitly measured, bounded exposure, using matched-framing and repeated-turn tests appropriate to the target substrate and domain; Section 3.1's lawful-reconciliation metrics alone cannot measure proposal-distribution bias. The appropriate choice depends on stakes, observed coupling, domain law, and acceptable residual risk. High-consequence contexts may warrant separation before any in-model protection is trusted, but this is a risk-sensitive architectural choice, not a universal empirical verdict in advance of qualification. What cannot be claimed is that commit-authority separation makes a shared proposer contamination-free. It is commit-clean only where its commits actually satisfy each square's gates, and proposal exposure remains a separate, unresolved ingress concern.

The proposer-sharing pattern answers a frequent implementation question: distinct squares do not logically require distinct model weights or vendors. A model may be invoked separately under isolated per-square contexts; a genuinely shared context introduces proposal-level exposure that needs its own qualification and risk decision. Commit authority, however, cannot be shared *as a single authority deciding for both squares* if the design is to exercise genuine sovereign separation. The federation requirement is that each square retains independent jurisdiction over its own commits. Whether proposer infrastructure can be shared safely is a substrate- and domain-conditioned empirical question.

## 5. The Three-Layer Structure

A three-layer rendering structure emerges from the worked example and generalizes to all federations.

**Layer one: committed state in each square.** Each square commits its own state under its own commit authority. ChaOS commits character state under the DM's character-running authority. WorldState commits world state under the DM's worldbuilder authority — held by the same person in the tabletop case, but potentially by different people in many other federation cases, and it is precisely that potential separation, as Section 2 notes, that turns a pre-federation into a federation proper. The state at this layer is canonical, governed, audited, and sovereign within its square's domain.

**Layer two: governed inter-square handoff at the canonical-dictionary boundary.** The negotiated canonical dictionary specifies deterministic address and representation mappings *where those mappings are meaningful*. But a mapping does not establish permission to disclose or a receiving square's authority to adopt what arrives. A lawful handoff has separable questions: (1) does the source square authorize this disclosure under its own law, including observability, confidentiality, and any necessary consent or evidentiary conditions; (2) what negotiated translation maps the authorized content to the receiving representation; and (3) under what receiving-domain admissibility and epistemic rules may the content become an input to a proposal or state transition? For example, a returning character may lawfully perceive only visible features of a location, not every WorldState field; a clinical-to-legal handoff may carry only information authorized by the applicable disclosure and privilege rules. The receiving square's own writer remains the sole authority for its durable commits. Deterministic form rewriting is a candidate for early Stage 3 promotion after its mappings are agreed and tested; admissibility can be deterministic only where the domain experts have actually rendered a decidable rule. Contextual judgments such as perceptual salience are not silently made deterministic by the mapping. At this boundary, measure the triggered consultation and reconciliation duties described in Section 3.1 rather than assuming every divergence is drift.

**Layer three: human rendering of the cross-square state into the medium the participants actually consume.** The DM's narrative description at the table. The clinician's recommendation at the bedside. The attorney's analysis at the court. The scientist's publication at the journal. This layer is where domain expertise manifests as practice. The federation makes this layer's work tractable by ensuring that the underlying state is governed and coherent across all the squares the practice draws on, rather than being improvised at every cross-domain handoff.

The three layers map onto OTTC's existing concepts with distinct authorities. Layer one is the per-square commit pipeline, governed by the rectangle's functional invariants. Layer two comprises source authorization, negotiated translation, and receiving admissibility; deterministic parts may be promoted to external enforcement when qualified, while judgment-dependent parts remain under appropriately authorized domain governance. Layer three is the human rendering of the lawful cross-square outcome into professional or narrative practice. Neither the translator nor the shared proposer acquires an independent right to write another square's state.

This structure is general. Whatever the squares are, whatever the domain expertise involved, federation produces the same three layers. The worked example happens to be narrative; the structure is not.

## 6. Scaling Upward — Paradigm Cases

The federation pattern operates the same way at higher-stakes registers. The commit authorities are different. The lawful structures are different. The proper-use boundaries are different. The pattern is the same. These cases are also, as Section 2 established, where genuine federation — two distinct sovereign authorities — begins, and where the sovereign-separation axis that the tabletop pre-federation cannot exercise is finally put to the test.

**Clinical-legal federation.** A clinical square commits clinical state under physician commit authority. A legal square commits legal state under attorney commit authority. The canonical dictionary handles boundary referents — *consent*, *capacity*, *negligence*, *standard of care* — with explicit recognition that these may be partially shared, partially divergent, or entirely non-translatable. Clinical "consent" and legal "consent" overlap in places and diverge in others; clinical consent involves a clinical-encounter speech-act between provider and patient governed by capacity assessment and disclosure standards, while legal consent is a jurisdictional construct shaped by tort doctrine and contract law. The federation does not collapse these into a single field. It marks the divergences explicitly, with translation rules that handle the cases where the concepts genuinely overlap and explicit non-translatability markers for the cases where they do not. This is the first case where two authorities can genuinely disagree — a physician and an attorney can hold incompatible commitments about what "consent" was established — and the negotiation of the dictionary entry is exactly the sovereign-separation work the tabletop case never had to do. Lazy evaluation at this boundary: legal state does not propagate continuously into clinical decisions; it updates at the boundary moments where the clinical context actually consults legal state. Proposer sharing: the same LLM may propose for both squares with separate commit authorities — subject, given the genuine capacity of the two framings to corrupt each other, to the context-isolation discipline of Section 4, since a legal framing bleeding into clinical proposal-generation is exactly the kind of high-consequence contamination that warrants isolation rather than mere monitoring. Three-layer rendering: committed state in each square, deterministic translation at the boundary, human rendering at the practice surface — the clinician with the patient, the attorney with the client.

**Scientific-policy federation.** A scientific square commits research state under researcher commit authority. A policy square commits policy state under policy-maker commit authority. The canonical dictionary handles boundary referents — *evidence*, *uncertainty*, *causal claim*, *recommendation* — with explicit translation work needed because scientific evidence standards and policy evidence standards are not the same. A scientific finding does not translate directly into policy authority; the translation requires further work that neither square alone can do, often involving the negotiated translation of "what level of scientific evidence warrants what level of policy commitment" that the inter-domain expert pair must work out. The federation marks this explicitly rather than collapsing the standards. The lazy-evaluation, proposer-sharing, and three-layer-rendering patterns all apply, the proposer-sharing one again under the Section 4 context-isolation caveat where the two evidentiary framings can contaminate each other.

**Ecological-economic federation.** An ecological square commits ecological state under ecologist commit authority. An economic square commits economic state under economist commit authority. The canonical dictionary handles boundary referents — *resource*, *value*, *externality*, *sustainability* — with explicit recognition that these are constituted differently in each domain. "Sustainability" in ecological terms is not the same construct as "sustainability" in economic terms; the surface word is shared, the underlying concept is contested between the disciplines, and the federation must mark this contestation explicitly rather than papering over it.

The pattern is proposed to be reusable across domains: sovereignty per square, negotiated boundary translation, explicit admissibility, perspectivism where appropriate, lazy evaluation where justified, bounded proposer sharing, opportunity-conditioned reconciliation measures, and domain-level rendering. These are design requirements and research hypotheses, not yet a validated multi-authority implementation. What remains unproven is whether separate sovereign authorities can sustain lawful information exchange and independent commits over long horizons under actual substrate pressure. The tabletop case offers an operational human precedent; qualification of the specified machinery and the sovereign-separation axis remains to be done.

A note on the inter-domain expert-pair work that high-stakes federations require. The canonical-dictionary entries between two consequence-bearing squares cannot be drafted by either domain expert alone. They are negotiated work. Two experts working together identify which referents are genuinely shared, which only appear shared, which require translation rules, which are non-translatable and must be marked as such. This is the research field that does not yet exist and that the corpus identifies as needed. This paper does not develop that field. It identifies it as the next layer of work that emerges once the federation pattern is established and the first inter-domain pairs begin negotiating the entries that will eventually thicken into a creole of canonical-dictionary translations between consequence-bearing squares.

## 7. Hypothesis Machines

Once enough domains have rendered their squares and enough inter-domain pairs have negotiated their translations, multi-disciplinary teams can build instruments that hold all the domains' constitutional structures simultaneously while exploring hypothesis space within them.

Consider a prospective research team studying antibiotic resistance across pharmacology, evolutionary biology, public health, economics, and regulatory law. A mature federation could make the distinct evidence standards, lawful transitions, and audit trails available together without collapsing their sovereign authorities. Its outputs might include typed conjectures, testable predictions, evidence requests, and candidate cross-domain explanations. These are *proposals*, not automatically lawful conclusions: every claimed fact requires a warranted epistemic status, every cross-square transfer must satisfy admission rules, and each domain retains authority over what it commits. The point of the instrument would be to make hypotheses answerable to several lawful evidentiary exteriors, including observations that can refute or revise them, rather than to certify whatever the generator says.

This is the federation-scale application of the kernel's **hypothesis-machine** sense in invariant 10: epistemic-status typing makes mixed-provenance claims and predictive errors attributable, so conjecture, evidence comparison, correction, and potential falsification can form accountable cycles. A federation-based Hypothesis Machine is an instrument built on governed squares, not a separate meaning of "hypothesis" in which prediction disappears. Not every proposal is a prediction, and lawful processing does not guarantee a true hypothesis; the instrument must preserve those distinctions rather than obscure them.

This is not a near-term capability. The federation has to mature first. The squares have to exist. The inter-domain translations have to be negotiated. The institutional scaffolding for inter-domain expert-pair work has to develop. The sovereign-separation axis has to be proven in at least one genuine two-authority instance, which has not yet happened. The proposal-layer ingress exposure of shared proposers has to be either isolated or instrumented. Hypothesis Machines are the consequence of decades of patient federation-building, not a near-term deliverable.

The conceptual pattern has a strong antecedent at the tabletop, where game masters mediate character-world interactions under one human authority. The ChaOS–WorldState *federation* itself is still hypothetical; neither deterministic handoffs nor two-authority operation have been validated merely by describing how a DM handles a game. Moving to higher-stakes registers requires qualified squares, negotiated translations and admissibility rules, and demonstrations of sustained independent authority. Those are the tests that could turn the architecture's plausible mechanics into established results.

The promise of Hypothesis Machines is the affirmative answer to "why is this federation work worth investigating?" Mature, qualified integrations could make new cross-domain investigations possible in climate, pandemic preparedness, long-term care, environmental remediation, and other settings where evidence and authority are fragmented. The absence of sufficiently governed integration surfaces is a plausible contributor to these problems' difficulty, not a demonstrated explanation for why they remain unresolved. Whether the proposed instruments materially improve inquiry is a further empirical question.

## 8. Closing

The federation pattern is structurally specified, and the worked example makes its motivating human practice legible at non-scientific register under unified commit authority. It does not yet establish that the proposed cross-square machinery works in an OTTC runtime. What higher-stakes deployment introduces is both the need to qualify that machinery and the first genuine test of sovereign separation between two distinct authorities — an axis on which real federation can succeed or fail.

The work may be generations-long. Each square requires genuine domain authority over its lawful structure, and each inter-domain translation and admission agreement requires qualified expert-pair work. The relevant institutional scaffolding does not yet exist at scale. This paper does not claim federation is near or empirically proven. It proposes a tractable research and engineering path: specify the boundaries, test the deterministic components, qualify ingress and reconciliation under domain-appropriate conditions, and then attempt sustained two-authority operation. The labor required is recognizable professional and institutional work applied to a new substrate.

What this paper offers is an affirmative, falsifiable vision with its unproven axes visible. The corpus states the kernel and its testable hypotheses. The substrate physics paper supplies an engineering vocabulary. The bridging document on producing a square describes the method of ontology rendering. This paper identifies what a governed federation would need to do, what human tabletop practice can illuminate, what still requires deterministic implementation and empirical testing, and what new cross-domain instruments might become feasible if those requirements hold.

The next layer of work is the inter-domain expert pair research that the canonical-dictionary entries require. That work is the constitutional translation field that does not yet exist. It is named in the philosophy paper, in the bridging document on producing a square, and in this paper, but it is not developed in any of them. It awaits the practitioners who will take it up — the inter-domain pairs of experts who will sit down together and negotiate which referents between their fields are genuinely shared, which only appear shared, which require translation rules, and which must be marked as non-translatable. The corpus is the infrastructure. The squares are the early demonstrations. The federation is the next phase. The field that builds it is the work of generations, and it begins with practitioners who recognize that the work is available to them and that they are qualified to do it in their own domains, in collaboration with peers from adjacent domains who can do the same.
