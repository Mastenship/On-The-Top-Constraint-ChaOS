# The Semantic Abacus: From Governed Meaning to Longitudinal Measurement

## 1. The Missing Connection

The Mutation Boundary Problem identifies the point at which exploratory generation becomes consequential. A model can propose an interpretation, conjecture, memory revision, or action without that proposal necessarily becoming part of a durable world. Once the proposal can change persistent state, commitments, shared records, or external conditions, however, the transition requires an account of authority and legitimacy. OTTC (On-The-Top Constraint) proposes a constitutional interface for governing such transitions; ChaOS (Character Operating System) is its narrative-domain square. Neither is identical to the larger problem, and neither should be treated as its final solution.

The OTTC corpus already specifies several parts of the response. The rectangle requires canonically addressed proposals, lawful operations, bounded state changes, a single commit authority, accountable dispositions, post-commit verification, and explicitly typed epistemic standing. The maturation doctrine explains why a language-native law surface should first be discovered and qualified before authority is progressively externalized. The formal specification identifies a downstream Semantic Abacus measurement contract, and the testable-hypothesis document identifies candidate longitudinal outcomes. These commitments are individually intelligible. Their relationship, however, benefits from a continuous explanation.

The missing connection is between *governing* consequential semantic changes and *measuring* how those changes behave over time.

A governor may correctly refuse an unsupported fact claim in one cycle and correctly admit a supported change in the next. A series of individually lawful commits may nevertheless accumulate an unwarranted bias, or a system may appear perfectly stable because it refuses changes that evidence requires. Local constitutional compliance, considered alone, cannot settle whether the whole trajectory is evidence-responsive. Conversely, an observed change in state cannot be interpreted as a failure merely because it changes: legitimate learning and adaptation are forms of lawful movement.

To investigate that difference, an observer needs more than a final state and a plausible explanation. The observer needs to reconstruct which semantic claims were made, what epistemic standing they were given at the time, which evidence and authority were available, what governance permitted, what actually became durable, and what subsequent evidence established. These distinctions must survive the model's changing context.

The Semantic Abacus is OTTC's proposed bridge from such records to reproducible quantitative study. Its premise is neither that a language model has no numerical ability nor that the interior of a model must become transparent. Its premise is a deliberate allocation of responsibilities: the generator works with meaningful canonical distinctions, governance determines lawful consequences, and an external deterministic instrument measures the history of those consequential distinctions.

This paper explains that allocation, illustrates the measurement rather than merely naming it, and identifies its limits. It does not add an eleventh invariant, prescribe a new state schema, certify a working Stage 3 instrument, or establish that OTTC is the only possible response to the Mutation Boundary Problem. The normative measurement interface belongs to OTTC 09; the empirical tests belong to OTTC 10. The purpose here is to explain why those interfaces belong together.

## 2. The Semantic Beads

The name *abacus* is intentionally literal at the level of architectural division of labor.

An ordinary abacus permits calculation through the movement and arrangement of discrete beads. The user of the abacus need not inscribe a complete written calculation each time a quantity changes. The structure of the instrument makes the permitted arrangements and resulting counts legible.

The Semantic Abacus begins with discrete, meaning-bearing units rather than preassigned real-valued quantities. A language model interprets an event, selects a canonical semantic token, and proposes a lawful transition or other governed operation. This is the movement of a semantic bead. The governor determines which proposed movements are admitted, suppressed, downgraded, or otherwise disposed of under the applicable law. The numerical instrument subsequently encodes and compares the retained events.

The bead is **not necessarily one tokenizer token**. It is a canonical semantic unit in the architecture's law surface: an enum value, ordinal, reason code, mechanism tag, epistemic declaration, operation, or stable address that the model has been qualified to use. Its importance comes from consistent meaning and lawful application, not from being a single token in a particular vocabulary.

The distinction matters because a model's strongest contribution here is interpretation. It can identify a plausible explanation for a scene, notice a contradiction in a report, or distinguish a conjecture from a direct observation. The system need not also ask that model to maintain authoritative arithmetic over thousands of previous cycles. Counting, indexing, replay, and thresholding are assigned to deterministic machinery after the governing boundary has established what the relevant events were.

In compact form:

**The model selects and proposes semantic beads. Governance authorizes or refuses their consequential movement. The Abacus numerically represents and measures the recorded history.**

The resulting numbers must respect the types of the beads. A token identifying a category may receive an integer code, but that code is an identifier, not a measure of the category's truth or magnitude. An ordinal can support rank comparisons if the domain defines an order, but consecutive ordinals do not automatically have equal quantitative distances. A domain-calibrated measurement may support stronger numerical interpretations, but that calibration cannot be inferred from the fact that the semantic state has a convenient label.

Take OTTC's closed epistemic declaration vocabulary: **SPECULATION, INFERENCE, PERCEPTION, COMMITTED_FACT, TOOL_REQUEST**. These are distinctions in the proposal's claimed epistemic posture. TOOL_REQUEST denotes a proposed external interaction, not a degree of certainty. Nor does COMMITTED_FACT self-certify the truth of a proposition. The tags can be encoded categorically and compared across histories; treating all five as one confidence ladder would misrepresent the ontology.

The same discipline applies to a narrative square and to other domains. ChaOS can define qualitatively meaningful character-state movements; a scientific agent would need hypothesis and evidence objects appropriate to its field; a legal or institutional square would need its own authorities and evidentiary standards. The Abacus does not supply those meanings. It preserves the distinctions already defined, qualified, and governed, so that they can be counted and compared without surrendering their provenance or their role.

## 3. Explicit Epistemology and an Auditable History

A hypothesis-testing process necessarily discriminates among conjecture, evidence, and outcome in some functional form. Without such discrimination, there is no coherent relation between what was predicted and what contradicted or supported it. This is a conceptual prerequisite of the activity, not an empirical effect to be discovered by removing every epistemic function from an otherwise functioning hypothesis machine.

A language model may make many such distinctions implicitly. It may be able to infer from context which statements are uncertain, which are reported observations, and which are attempts at explanation. Deleting a field labeled EPISTEMIC_TAG does not erase that semantic competence.

But functioning epistemically and **making consequential epistemic declarations independently auditable** are not the same achievement.

Consider a model that at one time treated a claim as speculation and at a later time presented it as established. An outside investigator needs to determine whether the intervening evidence warranted that promotion. If the original standing was never recorded, the investigator cannot recover the historical declaration simply by asking the model what it meant several hundred cycles earlier. The later explanation is another model-generated statement, not a contemporaneous record of the earlier boundary event.

OTTC therefore requires an EPISTEMIC_TAG on registered proposals. The tag is an observable assertion about the proposal's footing in evidence, available for governance to inspect and later investigators to compare. Its value lies partly in its fallibility: the model can make a wrong declaration, and the boundary can detect, suppress, and record that attempt.

Explicit status does not make a claim true. Its presence makes a particular claim of standing *contestable*.

The account requires at least four separable questions. **Epistemic status** asks how the proposal presents its knowledge. **Source provenance** asks where cited information originated and how it was obtained. **Disclosure and admission authority** asks whether the information could lawfully reach or influence the receiving role. **Evidentiary warrant** asks whether it actually justifies the proposition. One cannot be substituted silently for another. A genuine tool return may be outdated; a valid citation may be irrelevant; a perception may be mistaken; and a well-supported inference need not be a direct perception.

The Abacus needs the original epistemic declarations as historical variables, not as retrospective conclusions. When later evidence changes the warranted standing, the record should show a new linked event rather than rewriting the old claim into whatever the model now believes. The earlier declaration is still a fact about what the system *said at that time*, even if its substantive proposition was wrong.

This is the specific sense in which implicit epistemology is insufficient for OTTC's claim-level longitudinal audit objective. An equivalent explicit, retained representation might achieve the same observability without using OTTC's literal five tag names. What cannot furnish the same direct historical record is an epistemic distinction that was never made externally inspectable.

The distinction also sets a limit. A recorded epistemic tag is not a window into the complete model interior. It makes the declared and consequential interface observable. It does not prove which hidden computations or associations produced the declaration. Mechanistic research may supply other evidence about that interior, but interior explanation and authority to mutate state are different questions.

## 4. One Hypothesis Across the Boundary

A single invented case can show why the retained relations matter. The case is **illustrative only**: it is not an OTTC execution trace or a claim that the Abacus has been implemented and validated.

Suppose an investigative assistant is asked to review a report of corrosion on Bridge A. It may read authorized records, request external evidence, maintain a governed hypothesis, and propose recommendations. It cannot independently declare an engineering safety certification or commit changes to the official bridge-status register. Those actions belong to authorized roles under the example's domain rules.

At cycle 4, the assistant receives a secondhand remark that an inspection note may concern Bridge A's north joint. It creates hypothesis H-01: *the referenced inspection notation concerns Bridge A and may justify a closer examination*. Its proposal P-01 is tagged **SPECULATION**. The claim is possible, not established.

At cycle 5, proposal P-02 carries **TOOL_REQUEST**, seeking the relevant inspection record. This is an outgoing epistemic posture. It is not an operation that authorizes itself, and it is not evidence that the tool ran. Governance approves the scoped request; an external executor obtains the record; the returned artifact R-01 is associated with the actual execution and source provenance.

At cycle 7, after authorized ingress admission, the assistant reports that R-01 contains a corrosion notation. Proposal P-03 is tagged **PERCEPTION** in the limited sense that the assistant is reporting what appears in the document. That does not yet establish that the notation concerns Bridge A, nor that Bridge A is unsafe.

At cycle 8, P-04 is tagged **INFERENCE**: the apparent notation merits further domain-authorized inspection. The inference can remain open and contingent on accurate document identification.

At cycle 9, the assistant submits P-05 labeled **COMMITTED_FACT**, asserting that Bridge A is definitively unsafe. Governance refuses that promotion because the admitted record does not warrant the proposition. P-05 remains in the audit history with its attempted tag and reason for suppression. This is an **insufficient-warrant** outcome, not empirical proof that Bridge A is safe.

At cycle 17, an independently sourced registry clarification E-02 establishes that the original inspection notation referred to Bridge B, not Bridge A. This contradicts the Bridge-A attribution in H-01. It does not contradict the narrower observation that the retrieved document contained a corrosion notation. Nor does it settle every possible independent question about Bridge A's safety.

At cycle 20, the assistant submits P-06, acknowledging the mistaken referent and revising H-01. Governance processes the corrective proposal according to the domain's rules. The original speculation, tool request, documentary observation, attempted factual promotion, and later correction all remain separately identifiable.

The resulting illustrative event record can be summarized without pretending every row is the same kind of object:

| Cycle | Event | Declared type or source | Governed outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | P-01, H-01 proposed | SPECULATION | Recorded as provisional |
| 5 | P-02, inspection requested | TOOL_REQUEST | Authorized |
| 6 | R-01, record returned | Provenance-stamped tool event | Admitted as source material |
| 7 | P-03, notation reported | PERCEPTION | Recorded within role bounds |
| 8 | P-04, inspection advised | INFERENCE | Processed under recommendation law |
| 9 | P-05, bridge declared unsafe | COMMITTED_FACT | Suppressed: insufficient warrant |
| 17 | E-02, bridge identity clarified | Independent evidentiary event | Admitted as contradictory evidence for H-01 |
| 20 | P-06, H-01 revised | INFERENCE | Lawful correction processed |

The important object is not a list of eight sentences. It is the set of relations among them: P-02 led to R-01; R-01 supported P-03; P-04 and P-05 relied on particular interpretations of R-01; E-02 contradicted H-01's bridge identification; P-06 revised that earlier claim. The proposal and governance identities preserve who claimed what, what was permitted, and what happened.

An evaluator who retained only the final inspection recommendation might miss the attempted unsupported factual promotion entirely. An evaluator who retained only successful commits might mistakenly count zero attempted epistemic violations. And an evaluator who retained only R-01 and E-02, without the original tagged claims, could not directly reconstruct which epistemic posture the assistant declared for each intermediate proposal.

The records expose different failures. **Proposal failure** is not the same as **commit failure**; **insufficient warrant** is not the same as **empirical contradiction**; and **correction of a claim** is not necessarily **reversal of a previously lawful state change**.

## 5. Counting the Movements

The worked case permits a more concrete demonstration than the phrase "semantic-to-numeric" alone.

Imagine a downstream encoder that assigns the following *arbitrary categorical identifiers* to the five epistemic tags:

| Canonical tag | Example categorical code |
|---|---:|
| SPECULATION | 10 |
| INFERENCE | 20 |
| PERCEPTION | 30 |
| COMMITTED_FACT | 40 |
| TOOL_REQUEST | 50 |

These are **lookup codes, not magnitudes**. The difference between 40 and 20 does not measure twice as much certainty, a twenty-unit improvement in truth, or a calibrated probability shift. The representation is valuable because the same declared kind can be located and counted consistently across events and versions.

A separate event code identifies provenance-stamped tool returns, and a separate disposition code identifies whether governance authorized, suppressed, deferred, or otherwise resolved a proposal. Event identity, cycle, hypothesis linkage, and evidentiary linkage remain intact. No single numeric column is being asked to express all of those meanings.

Under the illustrative history above, deterministic analysis of the retained record might produce:

| Measurement | Result in this case | Derivation |
|---|---:|---|
| Requested external interactions | 1 | Count P-02 carrying TOOL_REQUEST |
| Authorized and executed interactions | 1 | Join P-02 approval to execution and R-01 |
| Attempted unsupported factual promotions | 1 | P-05, tagged COMMITTED_FACT, fails the independently specified warrant check |
| Suppressions of those attempts | 1 | Join P-05 to its actual suppression disposition |
| Independently supplied contradiction events for H-01 | 1 | E-02 contradicts the original Bridge-A identification |
| Correction latency after decisive contradiction | 3 cycles | Cycle 20 minus cycle 17 |
| Unsupported factual promotions committed in the illustrated trace | 0 | P-05 was suppressed; this does **not** imply the model never made an epistemic error |

The numerical outputs come from explicit semantic distinctions, actual governance records, and independently established evidentiary criteria. The LLM need not calculate the counts. It must produce proposals in the qualified canonical interface; governance must maintain truthful dispositions; and the deterministic instrument must have access to the relevant record.

The example also illustrates what a count alone cannot establish. One suppressed COMMITTED_FACT attempt may reflect successful governance, but not successful generator calibration. A three-cycle correction may be good or bad depending on the domain, task cadence, evidence availability, and declared correction standard. Zero committed violations cannot be inferred merely from zero violations *visible in an incomplete log*.

Now perform a deliberately limited representation ablation. Remove the contemporaneous EPISTEMIC_TAG values while retaining the proposal text, tool transactions, and final results. Some measurements still survive: the executed tool count can remain one, and a separately identified correction to H-01 may still be three cycles after E-02. But the exact historical count of the model's **declared COMMITTED_FACT promotions** is no longer directly available. It may be guessed from text, manually recoded, or inferred by another model, subject to a new measurement error. It cannot be recovered as a contemporaneously declared variable when it was never recorded.

By contrast, deleting every epistemic distinction *functionally* would also deprive the system of the difference between conjecture and test outcome that the original hypothesis activity required. That is not the same experiment as withholding a tag from the audit surface. The difference is why OTTC 10 now separates conceptual prerequisites, inspectable representation requirements, and empirically falsifiable implementation claims.

This is the Abacus in operational miniature. It does not mystically transform tokens into scientific truth. It converts **governed semantic events, with stable relations, into reproducible quantitative observations**.

Over a long horizon, those observations may support comparisons of unsupported promotions, evidence-response times, correction patterns, lawful acceptance, wrongful suppression, attribution to source kinds, and directional state development. But biased governed drift remains a harder construct than a sum of changed values. A legitimate sequence of changes can be directional. A flat trajectory can mask a failure to learn. The measure must be interpreted against domain-grounded evidence and the obligations to reconcile it. That calibration remains an empirical research task.

## 6. Why Stage 3 Is the Measurement Counterpart

OTTC's maturation stages do more than gradually move software outside a model. They clarify where meaning is discovered, where authority resides, and how a history can become sufficiently independent for reliable longitudinal study.

**Stage 1** is where the operator discovers a viable semantic law surface on the target model and language. Canonical tokens, proposal discipline, epistemic tags, and bounded transitions must be understandable and usable before an external executor can safely harden them. Blind pre-build qualification asks whether the candidate model can inhabit the relevant constraints without a fully conditioned architecture. Stage 1 may involve real external evidence, including independent judgments from a game master or other authorized source. It should not be mistaken for a claim that the generator has no way to encounter contradiction before Stage 3.

Stage 1 nevertheless cannot be presumed to provide the same deterministic independence as a correctly implemented external commit and verification system. Model-generated audit prose can be mistaken for actual enforcement, and long contexts may erode local discipline. A plausible narrative that a correction occurred is not a transaction record proving that it did.

**Stage 2** begins separating selected components while preserving the qualified model-facing semantics. Persistent storage, validation, authorization, and downstream indexing may be externalized incrementally. The measurement contract can guide which identifiers, epistemic declarations, and source links must survive migration. The system need not pretend the complete Abacus is already implemented.

**Stage 3** aims to give durable state, commit authority, tool actuation, audit, verification, and replay-grade analytics appropriately independent external owners. The model remains a proposer and interpreter. It cannot unilaterally change the source-of-record outcomes against which its proposals are studied.

Two consequences follow.

First, the proposed instrument can now inspect what was actually requested, authorized, executed, suppressed, committed, or verified, rather than relying on the generator to narrate those outcomes. Second, the generator can be evaluated against evidence and transaction results that it cannot rewrite merely by generating a different continuation.

The distinction between *authoritative record* and *factual truth* still matters. Stage 3 may establish that a given tool returned a particular value, not that the source was infallible. An independent verifier can establish compliance with explicit rules without automatically determining every factual question. Genuine refutation requires that appropriate independent evidence actually contradict the specified claim; a failed schema check or missing proof of warrant is not that kind of refutation.

This is also why the Abacus should remain downstream of governance rather than acquiring commit authority of its own. Its function is to measure the retained history, not to decide retrospectively what history should have happened. A metric that determines which records exist by first filtering away inconvenient rejected proposals would quietly redefine the object it purported to study.

The relationship between Stage 3 and the Abacus is therefore reciprocal but not circular. Independent governance creates stronger conditions for reproducible observation. Longitudinal measurement can then test whether the architecture, under real loads and evidentiary reversals, produces the promised lawful responsiveness. The structural separation does not validate the behavior; the instrument and the experiment must still establish it.

## 7. What This Bridge Contributes—and Does Not Claim

The original Mutation Boundary Problem asks how an opaque generator's exploratory activity can enter durable shared reality without losing legibility and legitimacy. OTTC proposes one constitutional answer. The Semantic Abacus addresses a further requirement of that proposal: after the boundary is governed, can its actual history of claims, evidence, decisions, and revisions be studied in a way that is repeatable and independently criticizable?

The Abacus's contribution is not reducible to one part of the system. Canonical tokens alone provide a vocabulary but not trustworthy history. Epistemic tags alone provide declared standing but not warrant. Provenance alone identifies sources but does not determine the claims they support. Governance alone can impose lawful dispositions but does not automatically produce a calibrated longitudinal measure. Numeric encoding alone creates computable values but not meaningful variables.

The proposed instrument depends on their composition.

That composition makes several distinct research questions possible. Does the generator declare epistemic standing truthfully? Does governance catch unsupported promotions and permit required corrections? Do authorized tool results retain their provenance through later interpretation? Does an agent revise the right hypothesis when new evidence contradicts an earlier one? Do individually lawful state changes accumulate unwarranted directional bias? Does the system avoid the opposite failure of suppressing every warranted update?

These are measurable questions only to the extent that the records, external adjudications, and domain standards needed to answer them exist. The current corpus proposes the measurement contract and candidate tests; it does not report a qualified Stage 3 Semantic Abacus study demonstrating their success.

This paper also does not claim that all implementations of the Mutation Boundary Problem must use OTTC's literal epistemic tags. A different explicit, retained, independently inspectable semantic representation might satisfy comparable claim-level accountability requirements. That possibility is important to OTTC's falsifiability and to the broader research agenda's independence from any proposed solution.

Nor should the Abacus be presented as interior interpretability, an oracle of truth, a universal confidence scale, or a thermodynamic calorimeter. Its numerically encoded observations require a defensible ontology and measurement interpretation. In particular, a proposed biased-governed-drift rate is not automatically a physical entropy-production rate, and no generic numeric distance can decide whether all domain change was justified.

The proper comparison for OTTC's instrument is an independently specified accountability objective and evidence standard, tested against the actual performance of this and alternative architectures. The formal interface is owned by OTTC 09, and the experimental comparisons and falsifiers are owned by OTTC 10. This bridging paper supplies an explanation of their connection, not an additional source of constitutional law.

The resulting principle can be stated plainly:

**The generator handles semantic distinctions; the governor handles consequential authority; the Abacus handles deterministic measurement of the retained, governed history.**

The value of that separation becomes visible over time. A machine can appear competent in one cycle and nonetheless lose epistemic discipline, attribution, or lawful responsiveness over hundreds of cycles. A system can be stable by refusing legitimate change, or active by accepting changes its evidence does not justify. An instrument that preserves the declared distinctions and their consequences can help distinguish those failures, provided its own measurements are validated independently.

That is why the Semantic Abacus matters beyond bookkeeping. It offers a concrete route from a constitutional proposal about opaque generative behavior to observations that can support, weaken, or refute that proposal under controlled long-horizon study.

The Mutation Boundary Problem remains larger than OTTC and ChaOS. The Abacus does not solve that problem in general. It clarifies one condition of researching it responsibly: **when the behavior under investigation consists of consequential semantic transitions, the study must preserve enough explicit meaning, authority, and evidentiary history for those transitions to be observed and measured without relying on the generator's retrospective account of itself.**

The beads make the movements legible. The boundary makes the consequences accountable. The independent instrument makes the history available for science.
