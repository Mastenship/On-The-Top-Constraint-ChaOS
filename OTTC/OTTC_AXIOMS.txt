=== SECTION: AXIOMS_VS_RECTANGLE_RELATIONSHIP ===

[KEEP: Reading guidance]

This file lists fifteen axioms in terse form. The companion file OTTC_RECTANGLE_AXIOMS lists nine MANDATORY constitutional invariants in expanded form, plus several EXTENSIONS and the mandatory engineering qualification step. The two files are complementary, not redundant, and they operate at different levels of abstraction.

The fifteen axioms in this file are the framework's broader philosophical commitments. They include foundational claims about what the substrate is (SUBSTRATE_AXIOM), how meaning travels (RECEIVER_MEANING_AXIOM), and what governance pattern applies across human and machine cognition (HUMAN_PARALLEL_AXIOM). These are framework-level claims about the constitutional theory as a whole.

The nine MANDATORY invariants in OTTC_RECTANGLE_AXIOMS are the kernel of any specific compliant architecture. They define the minimal constitutional shape an architecture must satisfy to be OTTC-compliant: canonical addressing, propose-commit separation, single-writer state, explicit operations, hard-state versus soft-modifier distinction, step limits with override triggers, deterministic conflict resolution, audit closure, and post-commit verification. These nine are the rectangle.

A practitioner building or assessing a specific architecture should reference OTTC_RECTANGLE_AXIOMS as the operational kernel. A reader engaging with the framework's broader theoretical commitments should reference both: this file for the philosophical commitments that motivate the architecture, OTTC_RECTANGLE_AXIOMS for the invariants any compliant architecture must enforce.

The relationship can be stated compactly: the fifteen axioms include the nine, and add framework-level commitments that the nine kernel invariants instantiate but do not exhaustively name. An architecture that satisfies the nine kernel invariants honors the framework. The additional axioms here name what the framework as a whole is about, beyond what any single architecture's kernel can carry.

=== SECTION: OTTC_AXIOMS ===

1) SUBSTRATE_AXIOM
LLMs are meaning-generation systems; control enters through semantics before it enters through arithmetic.

2) EXPLORATION_ADJUDICATION_AXIOM
Exploration may be stochastic and plural; adjudication must be deterministic and rule-bound.

3) ROLE_SEPARATION_AXIOM
Separate ontology, transition law, mutable state, and governance authority into distinct layers.

4) WRITE_AUTHORITY_AXIOM
Most reasoning agents propose; exactly one authority commits.

5) CANONICAL_ADDRESSING_AXIOM
All state references must use canonical, machine-stable paths.

6) LAWFUL_TRANSITION_AXIOM
State may change only through a bounded operation vocabulary with explicit legality checks.

7) INVARIANT_AXIOM
Identity and continuity are preserved by invariants, not by style or narrative intention.

8) TRANSIENT_OVERLAY_AXIOM
Use temporary modifiers for short-horizon effects; do not rewrite baselines to express momentary states.

9) STEP_BOUND_AXIOM
Ordinal and enumerated shifts are step-limited unless override proof is provided.

10) PROVENANCE_AXIOM
Every committed change must trace to a proposal, a source, and a rationale.

11) AUDITABILITY_AXIOM
A system is governable only if its decisions can be replayed and inspected after the fact.

12) VERIFICATION_AXIOM
Post-commit verification is mandatory; no governance system is complete without a court of review.

13) CONFLICT_RESOLUTION_AXIOM
When proposals compete, apply explicit priority, tie-break, and veto policy before commit.

14) RECEIVER_MEANING_AXIOM
Meaning is completed in the receiver; transferability requires shared rules and stable protocol.

15) HUMAN_PARALLEL_AXIOM
The same governance pattern applies to human and machine cognition:
free deliberation, constrained decision, accountable outcome.

=== SECTION: OTTC_THEOREM_CANDIDATE ===

When semantic generation is constrained by lawful transitions, bounded authority, and auditable commit,
continuity emerges without requiring full observability of the generator’s internal manifold.

=== SECTION: OTTC_PRACTICAL_TEST ===

A system is OTTC-compliant if all are true:
- Canonical paths only
- Strict operation set only
- Propose/commit separation enforced
- Invariants checked before and after commit
- Every commit has provenance
- Verification pass can fail the cycle
