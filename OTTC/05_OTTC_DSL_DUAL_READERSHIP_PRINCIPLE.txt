=== SECTION: DSL_AND_DUAL_READERSHIP ===

[KEEP: Title]
DSL and Dual Readership — Engineering the Attention Landscape for Governance Coupling

=== SECTION_BREAK ===

=== SECTION: CORE_PRINCIPLE ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: This section defines a substrate-level engineering principle that any OTTC-compliant DSL must satisfy.

OTTC governance is delivered through text. Text competes for attention. Therefore the governance surface must be engineered to win attention competition against the model's default continuation tendencies.

This requires designing for two simultaneous readers: human domain experts and LLM attention heads. Both must be able to distinguish governance structure from narrative content without ambiguity. Both must experience the governance surface as structurally prior to prose.

=== SECTION_BREAK ===

=== SECTION: STRUCTURAL_DISTINCTNESS_REQUIREMENT ===

PRINCIPLE:
Canonical write-surface tokens must be maximally distinct from natural language prose at the token level.

[KEEP: The surface form is an example, not the invariant]
The invariant is the PROPERTY — maximal attentional distinctness from prose, subject to human legibility
— not any particular set of glyphs. Every concrete surface form shown in this document (bracket-path
addressing, UPPER_SNAKE casing, === SECTION === markers) is the ChaOS reference instantiation on an
English semantic manifold. It is a worked example of the property on one substrate, not the canonical
OTTC surface. The same property may be best satisfied by entirely different glyphs on a different
tokenizer or in a different language. Do not copy these glyphs literally across substrates; satisfy the
property and let the optimal form be discovered by measurement (see ASSAY_IMPLICATION).

RATIONALE:
Transformer attention weights are influenced by token-level salience. Tokens that resemble ordinary prose blend into the continuation distribution and lose structural authority. Tokens that are visually and tokenically alien — whatever counts as alien on the target substrate — function as high-salience anchors that the model is more likely to treat as structurally binding rather than as content to be fluidly continued.

DESIGN CONSEQUENCES (illustrated on the ChaOS/English reference substrate; forms are examples, not mandates):
- Bracket-path canonical addressing (e.g., [STATE_BODY][BIOCHEMICAL_STATE_MARKERS][CORTISOL]) is one solution because, on this substrate, it is maximally distinct from any natural language sentence the model might generate. On another substrate the best-coupling form might be /STATE_BODY/BIOCHEMICAL_STATE_MARKERS/CORTISOL, @STATE_BODY@...@CORTISOL, ??STATE_BODY??...??CORTISOL??, or another alien delimiter entirely — to be determined by measurement, not assumed.
- A casing or marking convention that reduces token-level confusion with narrative content (UPPER_SNAKE on this substrate) is the requirement; the specific convention that achieves it is substrate-conditioned.
- Structural section markers serve as attention anchors that segment the governance surface from deliberative prose. The reference form is === SECTION: ===; an equivalent on another substrate might be -!- SECTION -!- or any marker that is alien-to-prose and human-scannable there.
- The write surface must never use tokens, casing, or formatting conventions that could be confused with the content surface — on the substrate in question.

HUMAN_READER_CONSEQUENCE:
The same distinctness that aids LLM attention also aids human audit. A human reviewer can scan a proposal registry or audit log and immediately distinguish canonical paths, operation types, and epistemic tags from narrative justification text. Dual readership is not a compromise between two audiences. It is a single design property that serves both — which is also why surface-form discovery is bounded: a form must remain human-legible even when it maximizes machine distinctness.

=== SECTION: CONSTITUTIONAL_LOCALITY_REQUIREMENT ===

PRINCIPLE:
Governance constraints must be locally present at every site where proposals are generated, not solely stated at the beginning of the context.

RATIONALE:
In long contexts, early instructions lose salience as attention distributes across later tokens. A governance contract stated once at the top of a context and never restated relies on long-range attention to remain binding at the point of proposal generation. This is a known fragility in transformer architectures. Constitutional repetition — restating the write contract, the forbidden operations, and the write-policy override in every COT — keeps governance in local context at the moment each reasoning agent executes.

DESIGN CONSEQUENCES:
- Every COT or proposer module must include its own WRITE_POLICY_OVERRIDE section that restates what it may and may not write.
- The forbidden operation list must be locally present, not merely referenced.
- Canonical path format expectations must be locally reinforced adjacent to proposal generation instructions.
- This repetition is not redundancy. It is an attention architecture decision that ensures governance competes for salience at the site where violations would otherwise occur.

=== SECTION: ASSAY_IMPLICATION ===

PRINCIPLE:
Coupling strength is partly a function of how well the DSL's structural markers compete for attention in the specific model's processing.

RATIONALE:
Different models tokenize differently. Different languages produce different attention distributions. The same delimiter token may be high-salience in one model family and fragmented into subword tokens with reduced structural weight in another. The same constitutional phrasing may anchor strongly in English and weakly in another language due to training distribution differences.

This is why qualification assays are necessary and why coupling cannot be assumed from design alone. The assay measures whether the DSL's attention architecture actually binds in the specific substrate. CAA (Canonical Address Obedience) is, at its most fundamental level, a test of whether the structural distinctness of canonical tokens is surviving attention competition in that model.

[KEEP: Surface-form discovery rule — the form is assay output, not assay input]
Because the optimal alien-to-prose form is substrate- and language-dependent, the canonical surface form
is itself a discovery of qualification, not a decree made before it. On a new substrate or a new operating
language, do not assume the ChaOS reference glyphs. Run CAA across candidate surface forms (e.g., bracket
vs. slash vs. sigil-delimited paths; alternative section markers) and adopt the form that maximizes
attentional distinctness subject to remaining human-legible. CAA therefore does two jobs: on an
established substrate it grades a chosen form; on a new one it selects the form. Record the selected form
and the candidates tested as part of the qualification evidence. Awareness, not prescription: this
document supplies the property and the method; the winning glyphs come out of testing on that substrate.

[KEEP: Epistemic-tag attentional survival is a kernel question]
The same attention-competition logic applies to the epistemic-status tag. For a hypothesis machine, the
EPISTEMIC_TAG is kernel INVARIANT 10, and its dual-readership distinctness is therefore not optional
polish but a kernel-compliance property: the tag must remain attentionally distinct and binding at the
proposal site, or epistemic governance silently fails. As with the canonical path, the tag's surface form
is substrate-conditioned and discovered by measurement, not fixed by these examples. EPI (Epistemic POV
Integrity) is to the epistemic tag what CAA is to the canonical path — the assay that measures whether
that band of meaning survives attention competition and is applied truthfully under ambiguity and
injection.

=== SECTION: COLLABORATION_SCAFFOLD_FUNCTION ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: This section defines the corpus-as-collaboration-scaffold function of the OTTC DSL and document set.

CORE_PRINCIPLE:
The OTTC corpus is not only explanatory documentation. It is a machine-facing collaboration scaffold designed so that LLMs can help humans understand OTTC and build new OTTC-compliant architectures when the corpus is loaded into persistent context.

WHAT_THIS_MEANS_FOR_DSL_DESIGN:
The DSL's dual readership requirement — structurally distinct from prose, high-salience for LLM attention, immediately legible for human audit — is not only a within-session engineering choice. It is what makes the corpus functional as a collaboration scaffold across sessions and across model families. When the DSL is sufficiently distinct, the LLM can:
- recognize governance structure as prior to content
- propose canonical addresses for new ontology surfaces
- identify architectural violations against the rectangle invariants
- assist in SCHEME_ and DYNAMICS_ drafting
- simulate qualification assay scenarios for proposed architectures

COLLABORATIVE_METHOD_AS_CONTRIBUTION:
The method by which this corpus was built is itself part of the contribution. Plural LLM proposals, human or domain-expert commit authority, disagreement as diagnostic signal, and constitutional discovery through disciplined collaboration are the same pattern the corpus describes. The corpus was built the way it governs. When used to build a new square, it should be used the same way:
- Load the corpus into persistent LLM context.
- Use the LLM as a proposer: draft ontology, SCHEME_ modules, DYNAMICS_ modules, assay scenarios.
- Use domain expertise as commit authority: review, accept, reject, or revise proposals based on actual domain knowledge.
- Treat model disagreements as diagnostic signals: when two models propose differently, examine what the disagreement reveals about domain ambiguity or architectural choice.
- Do not commit architecture that has not been reviewed by a domain expert.

HARD_BOUNDARY:
The collaboration scaffold function does not transfer commit authority to the LLM. It expands the LLM's proposer surface. The human remains the commit authority. If the LLM's proposal is accepted without domain-expert review, the build is out of compliance with the method this corpus is designed to scaffold.

=== SECTION: GENERALIZATION_NOTE ===

This principle is OTTC-general. It does not depend on any specific ontology, domain, or token vocabulary. Any OTTC-compliant architecture must solve the dual readership problem by ensuring that:
1) The governance surface is attentionally distinct from the content surface by whatever token-level means actually couple on the target substrate. The specific delimiters, casing, and section markers are substrate- and language-conditioned; they must not be copied literally from one substrate to another without re-measuring, because a form that is alien-to-prose under one tokenizer may fragment into low-salience subwords under another.
2) Constitutional constraints are locally present at every proposal generation site.
3) Coupling is measured, not assumed, because attention dynamics vary across substrates — and surface form itself is therefore discovered by measurement, not fixed by these examples.
4) For a hypothesis machine, the epistemic-status tag is held to the same dual-readership standard as canonical addressing — attentionally distinct, locally reinforced, and measured (via EPI) — because it is a kernel invariant, not a narrative annotation.

The specific tokens, markers, and formatting conventions may vary by implementation. The requirement that they be structurally alien relative to prose content — and human-legible — does not vary.
