=== SECTION: OTTC_STAGE_MATURATION_DOCTRINE ===

[KEEP: Title]
OTTC Stage Maturation Doctrine
Why Stage 1 Matters, Why Migration Is Necessary, and Why Stage 3 Cannot Be Skipped

=== SECTION_BREAK ===

=== SECTION: PURPOSE ===

[KEEP: Purpose]
This document codifies the OTTC doctrine of architectural maturation.

It answers three recurring errors:

1) STAGE_1_DISMISSAL_ERROR
- “If deterministic external governance is the real target, Stage 1 is unnecessary.”
- “Just build the software first.”

2) STAGE_1_ROMANTICISM_ERROR
- “If the machine works in-context, externalization is unnecessary.”
- “Just keep everything in the LLM forever.”

3) STAGE_3_SKIPPING_ERROR
- “We can externalize immediately without first discovering the lawful semantic interface the model can reliably inhabit.”

OTTC rejects all three errors.

The correct doctrine is:
- Stage 1 is necessary for constitutional discovery and coupling assay.
- Stage 2 is necessary for safe migration and mixed-boundary engineering.
- Stage 3 is necessary for best deterministic governance results in long-horizon, high-risk, or high-consequence systems.

Stage progression is maturation, not repudiation.

=== SECTION_BREAK ===

=== SECTION: CORE_THESIS ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Treat this as the governing thesis of the doctrine.

A deterministic governance layer cannot reliably govern what the stochastic generator cannot yet lawfully express.

Therefore:
- Stage 1 exists to discover and stabilize the constitutional language of proposals, audits, paths, invariants, and bounded role behavior inside the model’s native semantic medium.
- Stage 2 exists to externalize selected authorities and checks without breaking the learned constitutional interface.
- Stage 3 exists to move durable state, commit authority, verification, and quantitative replay analytics into deterministic systems once the semantic protocol is stable enough to support them.

Short form:
- Stage 1 discovers the law surface.
- Stage 2 migrates the law surface.
- Stage 3 enforces the law surface.

[KEEP: Mirror clause — externalization also constitutes the generator's world]
The thesis above states the governing constraint in one direction: deterministic governance cannot
govern what the generator cannot yet lawfully express. The same progression carries a second consequence
in the other direction, made explicit in WHY_STAGE_3_IS_NECESSARY: once the generator can lawfully express
the protocol, externalizing the boundary does not only enforce the law — it constitutes an incorrigible
exterior the generator predicts against. Stage 1 discovers the law surface; Stage 2 migrates it; Stage 3
enforces it — and in enforcing it, gives the generator a world to be wrong about. Both readings are the
same progression seen from two sides: enforcement depth on one side, epistemic closure on the other.

=== SECTION: FOUNDATIONAL_OBSERVATION ===

[KEEP: Observation]
OTTC is language-native governance.

Because OTTC constrains a probabilistic text policy through semantic law, the model must first be able to:
- recognize canonical addresses
- distinguish proposal from commit
- obey bounded operations
- preserve hard-vs-soft state separation
- emit complete audits
- remain within epistemic and role limits, including truthful epistemic-status typing of each claim (kernel INVARIANT 10 for any hypothesis machine)
- produce artifacts stable enough for deterministic systems to parse

If the model cannot do this reliably, then external governance software will not be governing a stable constitutional protocol.
It will instead be parsing unstable text behavior and treating extraction fragility as architecture.

This is why Stage 1 cannot be skipped.

[KEEP: "Canonical" and "stable" are substrate-conditioned, discovered not decreed]
Throughout this doctrine, "canonical addresses" and "stable tokens" refer to whatever surface form
actually couples on the target substrate and operating language, discovered by qualification assay — not
to any specific glyph set. The bracket-path / UPPER_SNAKE / section-marker forms used elsewhere in the
corpus are the ChaOS reference instantiation on an English semantic manifold, an example of the property,
not the property itself (see DSL_AND_DUAL_READERSHIP). Stage 1's job includes discovering the surface
form that is alien-to-prose and human-legible on this substrate; do not import another square's glyphs
without re-measuring.

=== SECTION: WHY_STAGE_1_EXISTS ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: This section defines the purpose of Stage 1.

Stage 1 is the constitutional discovery and coupling assay phase.

Its purpose is not:
- final safety
- final determinism
- final reliability
- final production architecture

Its purpose is to determine whether the candidate model can stably inhabit an OTTC-compatible law surface at all, and if so, under what constraints.

Stage 1 is where you discover:

1) ONTOLOGY_FIT
- What persistent modules can be made legible?
- What tokens and record forms are stable?

2) DSL_FIT
- What syntax and segmentation does the model obey under stress?
- What canonical forms retain gravity over long contexts?
- Which surface form (delimiters, casing, markers) actually couples on this substrate and language?

3) COUPLING_LIMITS
- Where does the model drift first?
- Which invariants are weakly held?
- Which roles should be reduced, split, or externalized?

4) AUTHORITY_FEASIBILITY
- Can this unit serve only as proposer?
- Can it also govern?
- Must audit or commit be reassigned?

5) FAILURE_SIGNATURES
- Does it alias?
- Does it perform illegal direct writes?
- Does it narrate beyond role?
- Does it produce audit theater?
- Does it apply epistemic-status tags untruthfully under pressure?

6) LANGUAGE_AND_CULTURE_EFFECTS
- Does coupling change across intended language surfaces?

This is why qualification assay is a mandatory blind pre-build gate.
You test the ground before foundations.

=== SECTION_BREAK ===

=== SECTION: WHY_STAGE_1_IS_NOT_OPTIONAL_FLUFF ===

[KEEP: Rule]
Stage 1 is not “just prompting.”
Stage 1 is not “prototyping until software exists.”
Stage 1 is not “informal scaffolding.”

It is the only phase in which the operator can directly observe whether the candidate model can:
- inhabit canonical law
- separate proposal from reality
- preserve auditable causality
- and produce a parseable constitutional artifact language

without first being over-conditioned by a finished architecture.

A system that skips this phase risks:
- externalizing a bad ontology
- cementing unstable token choices
- building parsers for artifacts the model does not naturally hold
- mistaking conditioned post-build compliance for native coupling
- and investing engineering effort into a constitutional shape the model cannot support cleanly

Therefore:
Skipping Stage 1 does not save time in principle.
It often converts discovery debt into later architectural debt.

=== SECTION_BREAK ===

=== SECTION: WHY_STAGE_3_CANNOT_BE_SKIPPED_TO_IMMEDIATELY ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: This section refutes the “just start with software” objection.

The common objection is:
- “If external deterministic governance is the target, why not start there immediately?”

Answer:
Because deterministic governance still requires a stable semantic interchange format.

External systems must receive artifacts such as:
- canonical proposals
- legal target paths
- operation types
- reason codes
- mechanism tags
- epistemic-status tags
- audit entries
- verification inputs
- tool-intent requests

These do not arise automatically from generic model output.
They must be discovered, stabilized, and assayed inside the model’s own semantic medium first.

Without Stage 1, software teams typically end up doing one or more of the following:
- parsing prose that was never constitutionally stable
- inventing schemas the model weakly inhabits
- overfitting parsers to one model/version
- collapsing semantic distinctions because the interface was not discovered first
- treating extraction success as governance success
- confusing formal syntax with effective coupling

Therefore:
You may build external software early, but you may not validly assume that you have skipped Stage 1 unless the model-facing constitutional protocol has already been discovered and qualified.

The sequence is not:
- software first, semantics later

The sequence is:
- assay and discover semantics first
- then externalize deterministically

=== SECTION: WHY_STAGE_2_EXISTS ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: This section defines the role of the hybrid stage.

Stage 2 exists because migration from pure in-context governance to deterministic governance is rarely atomic.

Stage 2 is the hybridization phase.
Its purpose is to preserve the stable constitutional language discovered in Stage 1 while gradually moving authority, storage, and verification into deterministic systems.

Typical Stage 2 moves include:
- external state persistence
- external commit logging
- schema validation
- invariant checking
- canonical translation-before-commit
- tool-actuation mediation
- semantic-to-numeric downstream encoding
- role split across multiple models or humans

Stage 2 matters because:
- it exposes where the semantic interface survives contact with software
- it identifies which artifacts are parseable enough for hard enforcement
- it allows governance boundary placement to be adjusted by evidence
- it reduces the risk of losing substrate fit during externalization

A doctrine that lacks Stage 2 usually falls into one of two failure modes:
- indefinite Stage 1 romanticism
- premature Stage 3 overformalization

=== SECTION: WHY_STAGE_3_IS_NECESSARY ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: This section defines the end-state rationale.

Stage 3 is necessary because language-native constraint alone does not create hard guarantees.

Over long horizons, in-model-only governance remains vulnerable to:
- context erosion
- salience loss
- instruction interference
- role confusion
- audit theater
- accidental write-surface bypass
- adversarial pressure
- numerical brittleness
- weak replay guarantees

Therefore the best deterministic governance results require that, eventually:
- persistent state lives outside the model
- commit authority is externalized
- verification is externalized
- tool actuation is externally mediated
- quantitative replay analytics operate on post-audit canonical artifacts

Stage 3 is not a rejection of OTTC’s language-native nature.
It is OTTC completed at the enforcement boundary.

The model remains:
- proposer
- interpreter
- rationale generator
- bounded subjective processor

The deterministic layer becomes:
- source of truth
- commit authority
- verifier
- audit enforcer
- quantitative analytics layer

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: The enforcement rationale above is necessary but not complete. Stage 3 has a
second, epistemic rationale that the enforcement framing leaves unstated. Hold both: Stage 3 hardens the
boundary AND constitutes the generator's world.

[KEEP: The epistemic closure — independent authoritative boundary records]
Externalizing persistent state, commit authority, and verification does more than harden enforcement. It
changes the generator's epistemic situation. Once the deterministic layer owns the source of record,
the inputs returning across the boundary — committed state, verification verdicts, authenticated tool
execution/return records, and the next cycle's governed world — are no longer authored or editable by
the generator. To the generator, these are authoritative records of what the boundary did and what
external sources returned, not self-generated revisions. The generator faces an exterior it cannot
rewrite. A provenance-stamped tool return can nevertheless be stale, incomplete, or wrong about the
external world: independence of the return is not a guarantee of the truth of every statement inside it.

This is the condition that makes the propose→commit→verify cycle a real predictive-falsificationist loop
rather than a self-ratifying one. Falsification requires a test the conjecturer did not author; predictive
processing requires a corrective signal the predictor does not control. Both demand an exterior. Stage 3
is that exterior:
- Each proposal — especially each COMMITTED_FACT — is a conjecture and a prediction about what should
  become durable state.
- The externalized commit and verification return the actual outcome — what did become state, what the
  evidence permitted — from outside the generator.
- The gap between proposed and committed is an externally determined governance-outcome difference,
  which can be measured independently of the generator. It can inform predictive correction, but is
  not necessarily empirical prediction error about external facts.
- A verification FAIL on a COMMITTED_FACT is a typed accountability signal. It may record illegal
  operation, insufficient evidentiary warrant, or actual independent contradiction of a specified
  factual hypothesis. Only the latter is empirical refutation. Kernel INVARIANT 10 permits the
  failure to be attributed to the originally declared epistemic standing rather than left undifferentiated.
- The next cycle's inputs are the updated world the generator must now predict against.

Therefore Stage 3 permits one externally governed evidence-and-commit history to support both
predict→compare→update and conjecture→test→refute when the recorded event actually supplies a test
of the specified hypothesis. The resulting loop can be independently measured instead of merely
narrated by its proposer. Stages 1 and 2 can encounter genuine external contradictions — for
example, an independently governed game master's world event — but cannot guarantee separation
of all model-operated commit and verification surfaces. Stage 3 systematically hardens that
independence and makes the complete governed history available for deterministic replay. The move
to proposer-only authority is both an enforcement move and an epistemic move: the generator must
respond to consequences it did not have the power to author.

[KEEP: Status of the closure — independent records structurally guaranteed; correction empirically tested]
A correctly implemented Stage 3 boundary makes recorded commit, verification, and tool-execution
outcomes independent of the generator's unilateral authorship. That is a structural property of the
authority split, not proof that each source claim is true or that the generator responds correctly.
Whether a given generator reliably updates its hypotheses and proposals in response to the feedback,
rather than confabulating around it under pressure, remains a coupling question: measure, do not
assume (RULE_3, RULE_4). The long-horizon, post-audit Semantic Abacus is the proposed instrument for
measuring evidentiary correction and the attribution of failed claims across cycles and context loads.
See OTTC/09_OTTC_SPECIFICATION_DRAFT.txt for its retained-event measurement contract and OTTC/10
for the experimental outcomes.

=== SECTION: WHAT_PERSISTS_ACROSS_ALL_STAGES ===

[KEEP: Rule]
Stage progression changes where authority lives.
It does not change OTTC’s kernel identity.

Across all stages, OTTC still requires the ten kernel invariants:
- canonical addressing
- propose→commit separation
- single-writer persistent state
- explicit update operations
- hard-state vs soft-modifier separation
- step limits with override proof
- deterministic conflict resolution
- audit closure
- post-commit verification
- epistemic-status typing

[KEEP: Cardinality note]
The first nine make the mutation boundary governable across every stage. The tenth — epistemic-status
typing — is what keeps the system a hypothesis machine (predictive processing + Popperian falsification)
across every stage, and is kernel for any architecture with mixed-provenance durable state. Stage
progression deepens enforcement of all ten; it never reduces the count. A maturation step that drops an
invariant is not maturation — it is regression.

What changes is:
- whether these are represented entirely in-model
- partially externalized
- or enforced by deterministic source-of-truth systems

[KEEP: Progression changes where authority lives and what the generator's inputs are]
Stage progression changes where authority lives; at the terminal stage it also changes what the
generator's inputs are. In-model, the generator's "world" is partly its own representation, so the
predictive-falsificationist loop runs against a partially self-authored exterior. As state, commit, and
verification externalize, the generator's inputs become progressively incorrigible to it, until at Stage 3
they are world-truth in full (see WHY_STAGE_3_IS_NECESSARY). The ten invariants persist across every stage;
what deepens is both the enforcement of those invariants and the independence of the exterior the loop is
tested against. Enforcement depth and epistemic closure rise together, by the same migration.

Thus:
Stage progression is not replacement of OTTC.
It is increasing enforcement depth of OTTC.

=== SECTION: THE_DEVELOPMENTAL_LOGIC ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Preserve this order as doctrine.

NORMAL_MATURATION_ORDER:
1) QUALIFY the candidate unit in a blind pre-build assay
2) DISCOVER the lawful semantic DSL / ontology surface
3) BUILD the machine in text-native form
4) RETEST the built machine for regression
5) EXTERNALIZE the state, commit, and verification layers where risk or horizon requires
6) PRESERVE the same constitutional interface for model-to-system communication

This means:
- the model learns to speak constitutional law before software depends on it
- software is built around a discovered stable protocol rather than around wishful schema design
- deterministic governance receives canonical artifacts rather than unstable prose

=== SECTION: STAGE_1_FAILURE_MODE ===

[KEEP: Failure Mode]
If an architecture remains indefinitely in Stage 1 while:
- authority grows
- consequence grows
- horizon grows
- adversarial pressure grows

then governance debt is accumulating.

Symptoms:
- more law is added to compensate for missing enforcement
- audit grows theatrical under pressure
- context burden rises
- deterministic guarantees are claimed without deterministic authority
- operators begin trusting conditioned behavior as if it were runtime enforcement

Remedy:
- Migrate selected authorities into Stage 2/3 deterministic governance.

=== SECTION_BREAK ===

=== SECTION: STAGE_3_PREMATURE_FORMALIZATION_FAILURE_MODE ===

[KEEP: Failure Mode]
If an architecture attempts Stage 3 externalization before constitutional discovery is complete, common failures include:
- parser brittleness
- schema mismatch
- unstable canonical tokens
- overfit prompts bound to one model/version
- loss of domain nuance at the machine boundary
- governance software that enforces malformed abstractions
- expensive code commitments to a protocol the model never truly inhabited

Remedy:
- Return to Stage 1 qualification and DSL assay.
- Re-establish the lawful semantic protocol before hardening the deterministic boundary.

=== SECTION: REMEDIATION_REACHABILITY_ACROSS_STAGES ===

[KEEP: Safety, lawful continuity, and domain-owned recovery]

A governance architecture may successfully prohibit unlawful mutation while also preventing every lawful route to further operation. This is a containment deadlock: the boundary preserves its prohibitions, but the architecture has failed to specify a reachable, authorized disposition of the exceptional state.

This failure becomes more consequential during stage maturation. In Stage 1, an LLM or human operator may informally compensate for an underspecified recovery path. Such compensation is not evidence that the architecture contains a lawful recovery mechanism. At Stage 3, deterministic enforcement will apply the specified restriction without inventing an exception. An unresolved ambiguity can therefore become an indefinitely enforced deadlock.

The general maturation obligation is REMEDIATION_REACHABILITY: every exceptional containment state must have a defined, domain-authorized route to lawful disposition.

Lawful disposition does not necessarily mean restoration of ordinary operation. Depending on domain, severity, evidence, and governing authority, it may mean verified repair and resumption, continued operation on independently safe surfaces, authorized human review, controlled suspension pending evidence, or accountable termination of the affected process.

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Treat REMEDIATION_REACHABILITY as stage-maturation and domain-architecture guidance. It does not add an OTTC kernel invariant, prescribe one universal quarantine policy, or authorize automatic release from unresolved integrity failures.

REQUIRED_RECOVERY_CONTRACT:
- CONTAINMENT_SCOPE: Identify the compromised state surfaces, affected dependencies, and conditions that justify exceptional containment.
- INDEPENDENT_PROGRESS: Specify whether unrelated lawful transitions can remain admissible. Do not impose global suppression when the affected integrity can be safely isolated.
- LAWFUL_DISPOSITION: Define the domain-permitted routes to repair, partial operation, escalation, continued safe hold, or termination.
- AUTHORITY: Identify who or what may initiate, approve, perform, and confirm each recovery action. Human intervention is subject to the domain's actual authority and accountability requirements.
- RELEASE_EVIDENCE: Define what evidence permits containment to be lifted. Ending a temporary containment interval does not establish that the underlying fault has been resolved.
- AUDIT_AND_VERIFICATION: Preserve the triggering reasons, authorized recovery actions, evidence, resulting state, and verification outcome. Recovery must not create an unrecorded mutation.
- GOVERNOR_FAILURE: If the ordinary commit machinery is itself untrustworthy or unavailable, use a separately authorized and auditable recovery procedure. Do not require repair through a mechanism whose integrity cannot be established, and do not permit an ungoverned bypass.
- UNRESOLVED_CASE: When safe repair or resumption cannot be justified, preserve the appropriate safe state and expose an accountable route for review or escalation. Do not convert lack of evidence into automatic permission.

STAGE_1:
- Discover the domain's actual containment and recovery requirements.
- Identify where the human operator is silently supplying missing recovery judgment.
- Test scenarios in which ordinary proposals are blocked but independently lawful progress should remain possible.

STAGE_2:
- Translate recovery conditions, affected dependencies, authority roles, and disposition records into explicit machine-legible procedures.
- Test the interaction between semantic recovery instructions and external validation.
- Retest recovery when authority placement, artifact shape, or enforcement behavior changes.

STAGE_3:
- Verify that every implemented containment state has a defined, authorized disposition route under stated assumptions.
- Test blocked repair, repeated failure, unavailable authority, compromised commit machinery, and unresolved external effects.
- Preserve safe suspension when no lawful resumption condition is satisfied.
- Never substitute elapsed time, operator convenience, or a generic PASS result for required evidence of structural repair.

DOMAIN_BOUNDARY:
- ChaOS may use bounded exceptional containment and continued narrative participation because those choices follow from its character-continuity requirements and Dungeon Master authority.
- Other domains may require indefinite safe suspension, multiple independent approvals, external repair, or permanent termination.
- OTTC requires the recovery boundary to remain explicit, lawful, and accountable. The domain's square determines which dispositions are legitimate.

=== SECTION: PRACTICAL_OPERATOR_RULES ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: These rules are actionable and normative.

RULE_1:
Never treat post-build regression evidence as qualification evidence.

RULE_2:
Never claim Stage 3 governance if the model-facing constitutional interface was not first assayed and stabilized.

RULE_3:
Never confuse parseability with coupling.
A parser can extract unstable behavior; that does not make the protocol sound.

RULE_4:
Never confuse Stage 1 success with final safety.
In-model compliance is not deterministic enforcement.

RULE_5:
Never skip Stage 2 if migration changes artifact shape, authority placement, or verification behavior.
Hybridization exists to protect protocol continuity.

RULE_6:
As stakes rise, externalize:
- persistent state
- commit authority
- verification
- tool actuation
- downstream numeric analytics

RULE_7:
Preserve the constitutional language across migration.
Do not let software erase the semantic distinctions that made governance possible.

RULE_8:
Preserve remediation reachability across stage maturation.
- Before externalizing or changing a containment mechanism, identify its domain-authorized routes to repair, partial lawful operation, accountable escalation, safe suspension, or termination.
- Test those routes under failure conditions, not merely the system's ability to reject invalid proposals.
- Do not replace a missing recovery procedure with an automatic fail-open, an ungoverned human bypass, or an unsupported declaration that the fault is resolved.
- A domain may legitimately forbid resumption until required evidence or approval exists. Such a hold must remain explicit and accountable, not disappear into silent architectural deadlock.
- Apply REMEDIATION_REACHABILITY_ACROSS_STAGES.

=== SECTION: RELATION_TO_SEMANTIC_ABACUS ===

[KEEP: Clarification]
The maturation doctrine and Semantic Abacus are mutually reinforcing.

Stage 1:
- discover the semantic beads the model can lawfully manipulate

Stage 2:
- preserve those beads while beginning downstream encoding and comparison

Stage 3:
- treat post-audit canonical semantics as the source for deterministic numeric indexing, thresholds, trend analysis, and replay metrics

Thus:
Semantic Abacus is not a Stage 3 replacement for Stage 1.
It depends on Stage 1 having first discovered stable semantic units worth encoding.

[KEEP: The Abacus is the instrument that reads Stage 3 epistemic behavior over the horizon]
Because Stage 3 retains independently governed outcomes, the Semantic Abacus is more than generic
downstream analytics: it measures the proposed hypothesis machine's evidence-and-correction behavior.
Canonical, contemporaneously typed semantic proposals are the beads; governance decides the permitted
movements; external deterministic processing assigns numeric representations and compares the
post-audit event lineage across cycles. The original tags, evidence/provenance links, rejected proposals,
actual tool outcomes, and committed state transitions must remain available for replay.
Typed comparisons can distinguish source of error, warrant failures, genuine contradictory tests, and
lawful corrections. Numeric encoding alone does not prove refutation, truth, or drift; measurements must
be evaluated against domain evidence and independently specified reference outcomes.
Varying context load and horizon length tests whether the model remains responsive rather than
self-ratifying. OTTC/09 owns the measurement contract; OTTC/10 owns its empirical tests.

=== SECTION: RELATION_TO_CONTROLLED_TOOL_ACTUATION ===

[KEEP: Clarification]
The same maturation logic applies to tool use.

Stage 1:
- the model must first learn to express TOOL_INTENT as a lawful proposal artifact

Stage 2:
- governance mediates tool approval and logging with mixed in-model/external procedures

Stage 3:
- tool execution, provenance capture, and policy gates are deterministic and external

Therefore:
A controlled tool boundary cannot simply be “added by software” if the model has not first learned to communicate tool intent in a canonical constitutional format.
