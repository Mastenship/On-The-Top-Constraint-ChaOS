OTTC (On-The-Top Constraint) Systems — For DMs and Narrative Designers

=== SECTION_BREAK ===

=== SECTION: DM_PROBLEM ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Explain the narrative problem without turning a narrative square into clinical science.

If you're trying to run long sessions with an LLM as a player-character mind, the hard part isn't getting dialogue. LLMs can already talk.

The hard part is keeping a character:
- coherent across weeks of play
- recognizably themselves under new stress and hard choices
- bounded to what they could plausibly know
- free to evolve without randomly rewriting their whole personality
- wrong sometimes, but wrong in a character-consistent way

Most "You are X, act like X" prompts supply a portrait, not a lawful history of change. They may sound right for one scene and lose continuity later. ChaOS started as an attempt to solve that **player-character problem**, not as a model of clinical psychology.

**ChaOS is the character-specific square. OTTC is the more general rectangle of governance rules later extracted from it.** The rectangle can govern many kinds of persistent systems; it does not itself contain a character sheet, a psychology model, or a GM.

=== SECTION: DM_TERMS ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Translate OTTC into tabletop practice without promising deterministic model behavior.

Think of OTTC as the **rules for what gets written on a persistent sheet**, who may authorize a change, and what evidence supports the change. Think of ChaOS as one playable rendering for a character: psychology-informed narrative concepts, qualitative state changes, temporary pressures, and viewpoint-limited cognition.

The distinction is familiar to a GM:
- **Proposal:** "My character tries to persuade the guard."
- **Adjudication:** The DM decides what happens in the world.
- **Commit:** The authorized state changes are entered into the continuing record.
- **Verification:** The group or a separate checker can notice whether the update violated an established rule.
- **Epistemic status:** "I saw the guard hesitate" is different from "I think the guard is afraid" and different again from "The guard is definitely working for the duke."

A model's fluent narration is not the authority to resolve the world. In-model constraints remain probabilistic; the GM's judgment and, where built, deterministic external tools supply firmer boundaries.

=== SECTION: OTTC_EDIFICE ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Describe the ChaOS narrative square and identify which claims are domain-specific.

1) A canonical character reality (a state sheet worth keeping)
ChaOS gives recurring character concerns stable addresses: BODY, SKILLS, RELATIONAL, MEMORY, GOALS, BELIEFS and others. Those are meaning-bearing **narrative control surfaces**, not measured medical traits. A field should not change just because the model uses a new synonym.

DM benefit:
- You can identify which lever changed and why instead of accepting "the vibes changed."
- The character can change and still have an accountable past.

2) Change control: PROPOSE → COMMIT
Stress, memory, goals, and other character-cognition passes may recommend changes. They don't get to rewrite the persistent sheet on their own. A single authorized governance writer processes eligible proposals under the square's rules. A verifier checks the primary outcome; verifier-originated governance corrections, when lawful, return through the **same writer** rather than becoming direct edits.

DM benefit:
- Personality growth has causes, limits, and an accountable record.
- The DM still decides what the story-world actually warrants.

3) Inertia and exceptional change
Some durable characteristics change slowly. Major transitions require a lawful override trigger and evidence the GM or constitution can defend. Step limits are a narrative tool for earned development, **not** a rule forbidding dramatic change when the domain's law permits it.

DM benefit:
- A betrayal can matter intensely without always changing devotion directly to hostility in one tick.
- A truly transformative event can still matter when its exceptional transition is authorized.

4) Tonight's pressure is not forever
Fatigue, fear, tunnel vision, masking effort, and momentary confidence may color behavior without silently rewriting long-term identity. ChaOS uses bounded transient modifiers to keep situational pressure separate from persistent baselines.

DM benefit:
- "She's snapping tonight because she's exhausted" needn't become "She's permanently hostile."

5) Player point of view — **the tenth invariant in DM language**
In ChaOS, a player-character can attempt, say, perceive, infer, speculate, and remember under their actual perspective. A character who spots a bloody sleeve has **PERCEPTION**; the thought that someone fought recently is **INFERENCE**; guessing the duke is responsible is **SPECULATION**. A source-authorized, evidence-supported fact may become **COMMITTED_FACT**, but not merely because the character is confident.

At the OTTC proposal boundary the complete canonical tags are: PERCEPTION, INFERENCE, SPECULATION, COMMITTED_FACT, TOOL_REQUEST. **TOOL_REQUEST** declares an intended external action; it is not automatic permission to act.

The name of the source (**provenance**), whether the source may reveal something (**disclosure authorization**), what the claim purports to know (**epistemic status**), and what actually supports it (**warrant**) are separate questions. The tag is not a magic truth spell.

DM benefit:
- The LLM does not get to narrate other minds or outcomes as established truth simply because it can describe them.
- Mystery survives. The character is allowed to be wrong.

6) A theory-of-the-situation ledger
ChaOS uses a domain-specific predictive frame: "What do I think is happening? How sure am I? What would change my mind?" That ledger is one way to give a character evolving hypotheses. It is **not** an extra universal OTTC kernel module; epistemic typing is the general requirement that prevents beliefs from silently upgrading into facts.

DM benefit:
- Misunderstandings become plot, and new evidence can change beliefs without erasing continuity.

7) Auditability: a DM's receipt book
The architecture distinguishes proposed changes, actual commits, truthful suppression reasons, and verification findings. If the model refuses every change, including a lawful required development, that is not robust governance; it's a frozen character. If the audit invents a justification, that's another failure.

DM benefit:
- You can investigate a strange character turn using an accountable record rather than a retrospective excuse.
- A trace is useful evidence but does not prove the model secretly reasoned as the trace claims.

ChaOS's current normal cycle contains **15 character-cognition priorities** followed by a primary governance pass, independent verification, and a restricted post-verification pass through the *same* writer: 18 execution priorities, not 18 different COTs. Consult 005_UPDATE_CASCADE_REGISTRY rather than treating this narrative summary as the runtime spec.

=== SECTION: TABLE_ENABLED ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Describe prospective narrative affordances rather than qualified guarantees.

**Long-form continuity.**
A governed sheet can make voice, attachments, risk posture, and remembered events more traceable. Whether a specific model retains these under campaign-length context pressure must be tested.

**Earned character evolution.**
A betrayal might produce a memory, relational shift, temporary stress, and a new hypothesis about loyalty. Those changes need lawful proposals and adjudication, not a single sudden global rewrite.

**Actual DM authority over outcomes.**
The player-character proposes actions. The DM or the authorized world-state authority determines the consequences. The character then gets only the evidence their viewpoint could legitimately acquire.

**Several characters, NPCs, and factions.**
Separate character sheets may share one human DM without constituting a proven machine federation. A separate WorldState square, independently owned by another commit authority, is still a proposed architecture. Crossing between character and world requires the source to authorize the disclosure, the information to be translated lawfully, and the receiving square to decide what its viewpoint admits. No one gets all the DM's secret notes just by entering a room.

**Narrative anti-fragility.**
Governed variability can be material for play. A stress state that produces a surprising but lawful character reaction is not the same as a direct write, secret omniscience, or missing audit. Those remain genuine failures.

=== SECTION: DM_LIMITATIONS ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Separate what is playable from what is guaranteed or qualified.

- ChaOS is **narrative** and psychologically informed; it is not a medical or psychological instrument.
- OTTC is not a magic roleplay prompt, a full RPG rules system, or a substitute for the GM.
- An LLM can misunderstand, forget, overclaim, or produce theatrical audits despite canonical files.
- The written architecture is a construction demonstration, **not** a qualified long-horizon production deployment.
- The single-DM table is a human pre-federation; it does not validate two independently sovereign machine squares.
- Stable-looking output does not establish long-horizon reliability, and a permanently frozen character is not a successful state machine.
- Genuine machine builds must follow assay-first qualifications before committing new machine ontology, then post-build regression. A prototype that was built first cannot retroactively call its later test a blind pre-build qualification.
- The ten prescribed invariants are not experimentally proven to be the smallest possible set. OTTC 10's deletion battery is still open.

The GM remains the authority for what is lawful in their game, even when LLMs help write the rules.

=== SECTION: DM_FRIENDLY_EXPLANATION ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Keep this short enough to say to another DM at the table.

**OTTC governs how a persistent system changes. ChaOS uses those rules to make an LLM character's changes playable.**

A character has a stable sheet. It cannot silently rewrite the sheet. It cannot claim to know things merely because the model generating its dialogue happens to have seen them.

The first nine OTTC invariants principally regulate the lawful act of changing the sheet. The tenth regulates what knowledge the proposal is entitled to claim as it changes it.

That's how a character gets to be mistaken without being omniscient, and to grow without being replaced by tomorrow's prompt.

=== SECTION: UNUSUAL_APPROACH ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Explain the square-first discovery rather than implying an ancient universal model came first.

Most people try to describe a character harder.

ChaOS began there, then tried cues, then a numerical design that proved unsuitable for the language-native workflow. Three Pillars made a qualitative character architecture possible. A reviewer later asked where the *governance* actually was; the DM had been silently supplying rules that the text didn't contain. Separating the growing ChaOS files by responsibility exposed the recurring governance requirements. **ChaOS was the square; OTTC was the rectangle subsequently discovered in its seams.**

The novelty claimed is a proposed synthesis and research method, not a demonstration that LLMs have biological psychology or that the character will perform flawlessly for every table.

A portrait describes a character. A governed character architecture says what the character is allowed to become, and how anyone would know.

=== SECTION: YOUR_DOMAIN_YOUR_SQUARE ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Preserve GM authorship and the mandatory construction order.

OTTC is a rectangle. ChaOS is one square built for narrative character continuity. You might also design squares for faction records, place histories, or an economy, but **different domains need their own actual lawful structure**. One GM's judgment can currently hold several ledgers at a table; two independent commit authorities introduce an untested federation problem, not a free interoperability feature.

HOW_TO_BUILD_YOUR_OWN_SQUARE:
- First, **check the ten-invariant kernel** and the candidate model/role surface. An optional prediction ledger is not a substitute for mandatory EPISTEMIC_TAG typing.
- **Before designing or committing a new machine's SCHEME, STATE, DYNAMICS, or COT artifacts**, follow OTTC 04 and 06: blind, fresh-session, minimally conditioned qualification in the order CAA (Canonical Address Obedience), WSD (Write-Surface Discipline), EPI (Epistemic POV Integrity), ANT (Audit Non-Theater). Record a GO/NO-GO decision.
- Only if GO, work with LLMs as auxiliary proposers to render your game's canonical addresses, lawful transitions, character roles, perspectives, and verification rules. **You** decide what the constitution may commit; the model doesn't gain setting authority through fluency.
- Use disagreement as a clue to what has not been specified. Ask the learner's question: "Where in the actual document is that rule?"
- Run post-build regression and longer-horizon trials. Don't call those tests the original blind qualification; don't count a model's approval of its own work as independent evidence.
- For a multi-square project, specify source disclosure, negotiated translation, receiving admissibility, and separate writers before claiming a federation.

You are still the DM. The models propose. The rules need to be *in the file*, not just in your head.
